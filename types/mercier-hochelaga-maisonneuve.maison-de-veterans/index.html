Veterans' house, to the Wartime Housing Limited model

Mercier–Hochelaga-Maisonneuve (borough of Montréal) · 1945–1976 (1945 – 1976) · Évaluation du patrimoine urbain (2005), sectors 27.U.1 and 27.U.4; Guide patrimonial de MHM, « Maison des vétérans » types I–III · Canonical: Wartime or veterans' house, 1½ storeys, to Wartime Housing Limited or NHA plans
Style: Wartime Housing / Minimal Traditional — Wartime Housing · Minimal Traditional

Veterans' house, to the Wartime Housing Limited model La maison de vétérans

Photograph pendingpending — the one Commons image of rue Curatteau was checked and rejected: it shows yellow-brick plexes at the north end of the street, not the 1953 gabled brick ensemble of sector 27.U.4

The federal minimal house, arriving in Mercier after the war it was invented for. A detached one-and-a-half-storey box under a two-slope roof, clad in pale clapboard or brick, built in whole streets to one volume so that the ensemble reads as a single gesture — around 1950 north of rue de Rouen, in 1953 on rue Curatteau.

Where it comes from

The Évaluation states the lineage exactly: these ensembles were built « autour de 1950 sur le modèle des maisons de la Wartime Housing Limited. Ce modèle a été développé durant la Deuxième Guerre mondiale pour répondre à la crise du logement qui sévissait alors et pour loger les ouvriers à proximité des entreprises consacrées à l'industrie de guerre. Le modèle fut adopté par la suite par plusieurs promoteurs qui cherchaient à rejoindre un segment de marché composé de personnes désirant habiter une maison individuelle à prix abordable. » That last clause is the whole post-war housing shift in one sentence: a state emergency type turned into a market product. The same object appears on this site at Lévis, in the Sud-Ouest, in Villeray–Saint-Michel–Parc-Extension, in Parc-Extension and at Verdun's Crawford Park; this is its eastern Montréal instance, and it is the one place in this borough where the cahier uses the word « modèle » of a house at all.

What Évaluation du patrimoine urbain (2005) says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • Grouped as whole developments rather than as single houses, laid out to one plan; the borough's ensembles are north of rue de Rouen and along rue Curatteau.
Massing
  • Detached houses of closely similar volume across the whole ensemble — « ces maisons présentent une volumétrie semblable, apportant une harmonie certaine à l'ensemble ».
  • Two-slope gabled roof.
  • The borough's Guide distinguishes three variants and notes that type II carries an entrance vestibule (« tambour d'entrée ») and therefore has no columns.
Articulation
  • Guard rails 36 inches high; simple painted metal fascia; painted or natural wooden slat soffits; painted untreated wooden columns where there are any.
  • Wooden surround mouldings, painted and untreated, around the cladding.
Openings
  • Double-hung windows in a 1/2–1/2 ratio, glazed in uniform clear or frosted glass without patterns, muntins permitted.
  • Door carrying one glazed panel, optionally divided into nine lights, the glass clear and plain, 91.4 to 124.5 cm high.
Materials
  • Light horizontal wood clapboard in a pale, low-contrast tone harmonised with its surroundings; brick with a two-slope roof in the rue Curatteau ensemble.
  • Original roof covering is asphalt shingle — stated as such by the Guide for both the maisons des vétérans and the Village Champlain houses.

Massing and context come from the Évaluation's sector fiches 27.U.1 and 27.U.4; everything under articulation, openings and materials comes from the borough's Guide patrimonial, whose prescriptive tables carry a « Maison des vétérans » column beside the secteur significatif and immeuble significatif columns, with three lettered types. The Guide is a conservation manual, so those lines describe what may be rebuilt today, which is the borough's reading of the original. Sectors 27.U.1 and 27.U.4 are not encoded in this page's sectors.yaml, which carries only the five E sectors of arrondissement 27; the U-sector enumeration is recorded in the header of that file and is Part 10b work. Note the date: the cahier puts these ensembles « autour de 1950 » and the rue Curatteau one in 1953, so they are post-war builders' adoptions of the wartime model rather than Wartime Housing Limited's own construction — « ce modèle fut adopté par la suite par plusieurs promoteurs ».
