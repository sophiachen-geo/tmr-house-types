Workers' house with carriage gate Maison ouvrière avec porte cochère

The 2005 cahier's own photographs of the I sectors — 24.I.7 at 1221-1229 rue Plessis and 24.I.9 at 1834-1848 rue Plessis, credited to the Ville de Montréal and its named photographers — are likewise not reproduced. A search of Wikimedia Commons on 17 August 2026 for a freely-licensed photograph of a Centre-Sud porte cochère returned nothing whose description survived inspection, and no substitute has been invented.

The eastern alternative to the ruelle, and the one detail in this borough that exists nowhere else on this site. Where the blocks around the Molson brewery, the Canadian Rubber and the MacDonald Tobacco works were cut into small lots with no service lane behind them, the only way to the back of the lot is through the building — so a full-height carriage gate is driven through the street front. Behind it are houses standing at the back of the yard and the coal and wood store. Two or three storeys, brick, vertical windows, elaborate cornices; the front setback is decided by nothing more than whether the stair to the upper dwellings is inside or out.

Where it comes from

The cahier gives the mechanism in the middle of its historical chapter, describing the dense population that gathered round the Molson brewery — in the quarter since the 18th century — the Canadian Rubber and the MacDonald Tobacco works: « Les quartiers qui entourent ces usines se composent de petits lots, et les quadrilatères (îlots) sont sans ruelle. Dans ce type de lotissement, c'est la porte cochère, toujours visible dans ce secteur, qui donne accès aux maisons en fond de cour ainsi qu'à l'entreposage de combustibles. » Set that against the Sud-Ouest, where Montréal's regulation of residential construction from about 1880 produced blocks with ruelles first in an « I » and then in an « H », and where the same regulation pushed the stair to the upper dwellings out of the building and into the front yard. Two solutions to one problem — how to reach the back of a deep, narrow lot — separated by a generation of by-laws and four kilometres of city. The carriage gate is the older answer, and Centre-Sud is where it survives; the RPCQ lists it among the characteristic elements of the declared site as well.

What RPCQ id 93528 (MCC); Ville de Montréal says about this type

Description (source)
  • « Les quartiers qui entourent ces usines se composent de petits lots, et les quadrilatères (îlots) sont sans ruelle. Dans ce type de lotissement, c’est la porte cochère, toujours visible dans ce secteur, qui donne accès aux maisons en fond de cour ainsi qu’à l’entreposage de combustibles. »
  • « Construit essentiellement sur le lotissement ancien, sans ruelle, mais pourvu de terrains très profonds, le cadre bâti offre une gamme de typologies d’habitation. […] La brique est le parement principal et la porte cochère est à l’honneur. »
Siting & landscape
  • Small lots on blocks laid out without a ruelle, which is the whole of the type's logic: with no service lane, access to the rear of the lot has to be taken through the building.
  • The carriage gate gives on to houses standing at the back of the yard and to fuel storage.
  • Deep lots, in 24.I.6 and 24.I.9, with rear-yard annexes since demolished; front yards where the ground floor stands clear of the ground.
  • The quarters that grew round the Molson brewery, the Canadian Rubber and the MacDonald Tobacco works.
Massing
  • Two or three storeys.
  • Fine cornices, and in 24.I.5 false cornices.
Articulation
  • Elaborate cornices, carriage gates and exterior stairs, in the cahier's own list for 24.I.6.
  • Elaborate crownings, slate roofs and dormers with worked joinery on the brick and grey-stone ensembles near rue Ontario (24.I.4).
Openings
  • Vertical windows.
  • The carriage gate itself — a full-height arched opening in the street front, still visible throughout the sector.
  • Access to the ground floor fixes the front setback: none where the access is internal, set back where a stair on the façade has to be accommodated (24.I.4).
  • « L’accès au rez-de-chaussée fixe la marge avant : nulle si l’accès se fait de l’intérieur, ou en recul pour accommoder un escalier en façade. »
Materials
  • Brick as the principal facing.
  • Grey stone or brick on the finer ensembles.
Éléments caractéristiques (source)
  • « La relation entre le lot et la rue se manifestant par la présence de marches, de portes cochères, de grilles, de servitudes de passage et de plusieurs entrées, à savoir une sur chaque façade donnant sur la rue. »

Roof form and pitch, roofing, lot width, setbacks and green front yard are null because the cahier states none of them. Roof form in particular is left empty on purpose: the I-sector fiches describe crownings and cornices rather than roofs, 24.I.4 records slate roofs with dormers on the finer ensembles, and 24.I.9 records a few surviving faubourg houses « avec toit à pignon » — three different roofs in one family, and no source that says which is the type's. The canonical form is given as the flat-roofed faubourg house because that is what the cornice-and-crowning descriptions imply, and it is the one inference in this record; it is flagged here rather than buried. No count, no dimension and no dated example is published for this type anywhere in the cahier.
