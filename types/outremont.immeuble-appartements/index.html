Avenue Bernard, Outremont (2016) — © Intermedichbo (Milorad Dimic M.D.), Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Three or four storeys of flats over the shops, and the reason avenue Bernard looks like one thing.

Where it comes from

The conciergerie is what avenue Bernard is made of. The 2005 evaluation attributes the street's harmony to a single fact — three-quarters of its buildings went up in the 1920s — and names the multi-family walk-up with commerce in the half-basement and ground floor as the dominant architectural type. The cinéma Outremont, given municipal heritage status in 2000 and since fully restored, stands on the same street.

What Ville de Montréal says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • Avenue Bernard between rue Hutchison and avenue McEachran, where 75 % of the buildings date from the 1920s.
  • Front setbacks of about 3 metres; the front grounds are most often paved rather than planted.
  • Also on the south side of avenue Willowdale, opened in 1911, and along avenue Van Horne, the working quarter's commercial artery.
Massing
  • Three or four storeys, multi-family.
Articulation
  • On avenue Willowdale, some display a strong Art déco influence.
Openings
  • –
Materials
  • Half-basements and ground floors generally occupied by shops.
