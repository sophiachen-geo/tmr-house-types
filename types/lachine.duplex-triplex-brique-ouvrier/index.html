Brick duplex and triplex, detached or in continuous alignment Duplex et triplex en brique, détachés ou en alignement continu

Boulevard Saint-Joseph, Lachine — brick and painted-brick buildings of two and three storeys in continuous alignment, with balconies, moulded cornices, a false-mansard turret and, mid-block, an exterior stair; the dome of the Sœurs de Sainte-Anne mother house closes the view. 876-898, boulevard Saint-Joseph, Lachine — a three-storey brick range in continuous alignment, its separate front doors at grade, projecting balconies at the upper levels, a heavy moulded cornice, and a raised central pediment carrying the date 1900. Photograph by Anne-Marie Dufour for « Parcours riverain - Ville de Montréal », 12 November 2010, via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:876-898,_boulevard_Saint-Joseph_(Lachine)_(8552645628).jpg

The worker housing the canal and the factories produced: two or three dwellings stacked on one lot, in brick more often than not, sometimes standing free, sometimes party-walled, sometimes running the length of a block in one alignment. Ville Saint-Pierre's have their stairs on the façade. Vieux- Lachine's sometimes have a carriage gate through to the back of the lot.

Where it comes from

Lachine's east end densified around work. The canal opened in 1825 and the first railway on the island reached the Lachine quay in 1847; Dominion Bridge settled near the locks in 1883 and other firms followed, and the cahier says workers arrived in numbers, settled in the eastern sectors, and pushed the built-up area across the railway. Companies and promoters, in its words, "construisent une multiplicité de types d'habitation et déclinent souvent leurs variantes en alignement continu, en série, jumelées ou individuelles" to house the factory hands and the men working on the canal. The porte cochère puts this record beside the Centre-Sud worker housing of Ville-Marie, where the carriage gate leads to houses at the back of the yard; and the exterior stair puts it beside every other plex on this site.

What Ville de Montréal says about this type

Description (source)
  • duplex et triplex détachés ou en alignement continu, le plus souvent en brique, avec parfois des portes cochères
  • des duplex ou triplex en brique avec des escaliers en façade
Siting & landscape
  • Detached, party-walled or in continuous alignment — the cahier records all three, sometimes in the same sentence.
  • In Vieux-Lachine the grid is orthogonal, the blocks rectangular and regular, and there are no back lanes; siting varies from hard on the street line to a setback deep enough for an exterior stair.
  • In the low part of Ville Saint-Pierre siting and setbacks are generally constant within a zone but change from one zone to the next.
  • Les implantations et les marges de recul des bâtiments, généralement constantes, varient d'une zone à l'autre.
Massing
  • Two to three superposed dwellings on one lot.
  • In Dawes the same fabric runs up to large brick buildings of two or three storeys with mansard or false-mansard roofs and dormers.
Articulation
  • Exterior stairs on the façade, in the Ville Saint-Pierre examples.
  • Sometimes a porte cochère — the carriage arch that leads to the back of the lot, the same device the Centre-Sud of Ville-Marie uses on its own worker housing.
  • The cahier records what has been lost as carefully as what survives: in Ville Saint-Pierre the wood cladding has usually been replaced by vinyl, and the cornices, crowns and railings have often disappeared.
Openings
  • The cahier states nothing about the openings of this type.
Materials
  • Brick, « le plus souvent ».
  • Wood, on the ornament that survives: the cahier notes decorative elements in wood on the two-storey houses of the upper part of Ville Saint-Pierre.
  • Les parements de bois ont la plupart du temps été remplacés par des parements de vinyle. Les éléments de décor tels que les corniches, les couronnements et les garde-corps ont souvent disparu.

Built from three sector paragraphs that name the same form in three parts of the borough, and from nothing else. The cahier gives no storey count for the duplex as against the triplex, no roof pitch, no window type, no lot width and no setback figure, so those are null; the flat roof is inferred from the crowns and cornices the same paragraphs describe as lost, and from the canonical forms this record is filed under, not from a statement in the source. The tenure plan is recorded as duplex because that is the smaller of the two the cahier names; the triplex is the same record.
