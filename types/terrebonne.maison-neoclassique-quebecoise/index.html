Neoclassical Québécois stone house La maison québécoise d'inspiration néoclassique

Maison Roussil, 870-872, rue Saint-Louis, Terrebonne (2021) — RPCQ id 92502, built about 1830, classed 1972 with an aire de protection in 1975. Photograph © Jeangagnon, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Maison_Roussil_-_01.jpg Address match: the uploader's description gives 870-872 rue Saint-Louis, the address on the RPCQ fiche for the classed maison Roussil.

The maison Roussil of about 1830 on rue Saint-Louis, classed in 1972 and given an aire de protection in 1975, is the anchor — a rectangular stone house of two and a half storeys under two slightly flared slopes, its front elevation laid in rubble dressed to look like cut stone, symmetrical and regularly fenestrated, with carved chambranles, quoins, a cornice on modillons, and wide two-headed cut-stone chimney stacks joined by a low wall at each end of the roof.

Where it comes from

Built by and for Noël-Théodore Roussil (c. 1798–1890), master joiner and militia captain, as both dwelling and craftsman's workshop — a door cut in the west elevation once gave onto the shop. The RPCQ reads the house as representative of the influence of neoclassicism on Québec residential architecture in the nineteenth century, expressed in the symmetry of the main façade, the regular ordering of the openings, the medium-pitched roof and the ornamental vocabulary of carved casings, quoins and modillon cornice; Roussil gave every interior bay a different treatment, which the fiche says is what gives the house its originality. As master joiner he is said to have taken contracts for work on the parish church and on the seigneurial office; as militia captain he sided with the loyalists in the rebellions of 1837–1838, and some authors hold that patriotes were held in the house awaiting transfer to the prison du Pied-du-Courant in Montréal. A second example stands a few doors away — the maison Alexandre et Joseph Roussil, built in stone about 1830 by Noël-Théodore's uncles "tout près de celle de leur neveu", two and a half storeys under a tôle à baguettes roof, cited by the Ville on 13 September 2021.

What RPCQ says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • Set on the edge of rue Saint-Louis, in the bourgeois sector of the Vieux-Terrebonne.
Massing
  • Rectangular ground plan, two and a half storeys, detached.
  • Two-slope roof of medium pitch with slightly flared larmiers.
  • Chimney and gallery are the projections the fiche records.
Articulation
  • Symmetry of the principal façade and regular ordering of the openings.
  • Carved window casings (chambranles sculptés).
  • Quoins (chaînages d'angle).
  • Cornice underlined with modillons; corbels; a wooden balustrade.
  • Wide two-headed cut-stone chimney stacks joined by a low wall at each end of the roof.
Openings
  • Rectangular casement windows; small-paned windows alongside large-paned ones.
  • Main door of wood, panelled and glazed.
Materials
  • Rubble masonry dressed to give the appearance of cut stone on the front elevation, over a stone foundation.
  • Cedar-shingle roof covering.
