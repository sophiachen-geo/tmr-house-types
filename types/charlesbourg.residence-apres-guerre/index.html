Post-war residence (bungalow) La résidence d'après-guerre

Photograph pendingPlan de conservation du site patrimonial de Charlesbourg (2016), fig. 32 — the plan's own photograph of 8191 Le Trait-Carré Ouest, the example it prints for this type. © Gouvernement du Québec, ministère de la Culture et des Communications. Not reproduced.

A rectangular or nearly square plan, one storey, a low pavilion or hipped roof, and brick mixed with artificial stone. Four lines is all the plan gives it — and yet more than a third of everything standing inside this declared site went up between 1925 and 1975.

Where it comes from

The bungalow is what the declaration of 1965 was reacting to. Sprawl reached Charlesbourg at the turn of the 1950s and cut up the farm lots; the plan records that 65 buildings, 48.5 % of the site, date from 1925–1975, and that most of the site's 26 single-storey buildings are post-war bungalows. The 2005 Étude de caractérisation makes the same point from the other side: by 1963 urban sprawl had already reached the ceinture, much of the agricultural heritage had gone, and the perimeter drawn in 1965 enclosed "le produit (la zone d'occupation villageoise) plutôt que la source (ce qu'il restait des terres agricoles à l'origine du village)".

What MCC says about this type

Description (source)
  • Au tournant des années 1950, l'étalement urbain amène le lotissement de plusieurs terres agricoles et la construction de bungalows à travers le site patrimonial de Charlesbourg.
Siting & landscape
  • Arrives at the turn of the 1950s, when sprawl subdivides the farm lots and bungalows go up across the site.
  • The plan counts 26 buildings of one storey, "majoritairement des bungalows érigés après la Deuxième Guerre mondiale", and 15 buildings clad in the non-traditional brick-and-artificial-stone combination, "principalement des bungalows".
Massing
  • Rectangular or rather square plan.
  • Generally one storey.
  • Low-profile pavilion roof, or hipped.
  • un plan rectangulaire ou plutôt carré
  • un volume généralement d'un étage
  • un toit à pavillon à profil bas ou à croupes
Articulation
  • The plan names no ornament for this type.
Openings
  • The plan gives no opening description for this type.
Materials
  • Mixed cladding of brick and artificial stone.
  • un parement mixte en brique et en pierre artificielle

Transcribed from the plan de conservation's printed p. 51. This is the shortest of the five type descriptions — four bullets, covering plan, volume, roof and cladding only. The plan supplies no openings and no ornament for it, and those cells are left saying so rather than being filled from the general literature on the Québec bungalow. The siting column carries the plan's own built-fabric counts from printed pp. 45–46, which are counts for the whole site.
