Cubique — massive symmetrical block, imposing gallery, low hipped roof L'architecture de style Cubique

Photograph pendingVille de Pointe-Claire, Code villageois PC-2880, chapitre 03, the figure captioned « Cubique ». The by-law's own illustration of the type. © Ville de Pointe-Claire. Not reproduced — permission required.

A massive, symmetrical two-storey block with an imposing gallery across the front, under a low hipped roof. It is the second of the two styles the Code villageois says rebuilt Pointe-Claire after the fire, and the Code describes it in a single sentence — which is exactly why it is worth carding: the record shows what a form-based code does and does not bother to say about a house.

Where it comes from

The Cubique arrives with the Boomtown, after the fire that took much of the village at the turn of the 20th century, and the Code sets the two side by side as the pair that "créent une certaine homogénéité". It survives in three of the ten landscape units, and the company it keeps in each of their style rows is worth reading: in UP5 it stands with Boomtown, Second Empire, mansard and vernaculaire industriel; in UP8 with vernaculaire industriel and Boomtown; in UP10 with Boomtown alone. Elsewhere on this site the same form is the maison cubique of Charlesbourg and the maison cubique of Gatineau and of the Île d'Orléans — a form that half of Québec's sources treat as a top-level type and the other half as a sub-variant of the industrial vernacular. Pointe-Claire's Code, characteristically, does neither: it treats it as a style.

What Ville de Pointe-Claire says about this type

Description (source)
  • L'architecture de style Cubique est définie par son corps massif et symétrique, sa galerie imposante et son toit en pavillon à pente faible.
Historical context (fiche)
  • Après l'incendie de 1900, deux styles principaux créent une certaine homogénéité.
Siting & landscape
  • Named as an architectural style of three units: UP5, the noyau villageois sud, where the front margin is very shallow and coverage about 40 %; UP8, the avenue du Golf sud, where the margin is deep and coverage about 30 %; and UP10, the avenue Cartier nord, where the margin is shallow to medium and coverage about 35 %.
  • In UP8 the Code says the deep landscaped setbacks are what compensates for a weak street frontage, and that the built form is homogeneous even though the styles are diverse.
Massing
  • A massive, symmetrical body.
  • A hipped roof of shallow pitch.
  • Two storeys, on the strength of the unit tables — UP5, UP8 and UP10 all give « 2 étages » — rather than of the style sentence, which gives no storey count.
  • Les toitures les plus typiques sont le toit plat et à faible pente pour les styles architecturaux plus récents.
Articulation
  • An imposing gallery. It is the one thing the Code's style sentence says about the front.
  • Across all village styles: a bandeau or crown on every heritage building; sober detail; railings of simple vertical members; shutters matching the style of the building.
  • Un bandeau ou un couronnement est présent sur l'ensemble des bâtiments patrimoniaux. Malgré les styles différents, ce détail participe à unifier les façades et à préserver le cachet villageois.
  • Les détails architecturaux sont généralement sobres. Les principaux sont les garde-corps composés d'éléments verticaux simples et les volets de fenêtre s'apparentant au style du bâtiment.
Openings
  • Regularly distributed and vertical, which the Code states for every style in the village rather than for this one.
  • In UP8 and UP10 the unit tables add dormers and balconies to the galleries as the sector's architectural elements.
  • Les ouvertures sont dans l'ensemble des styles existants distribuées de manière régulière et à la verticale.
Materials
  • The village rule: brick and darker colours on the chemin du Bord-du-Lac, clapboard or roughcast in light colours elsewhere.
  • UP8 gives « Variés » and nothing more; UP10 gives brick and wood clapboard in varied colours; UP5 gives roughcast, wood clapboard and marine tones.
  • Les matériaux et coloris varient quelque peu. La brique et des couleurs plus foncées sont typiques du chemin Bord-du-Lac alors que les autres secteurs ont souvent des déclins ou du crépi de couleurs claires.

The Code devotes one sentence to this style — twenty-two words — and that sentence is the whole of the description column. Everything else above is either a village-wide rule the Code states for all styles at once, or a row from one of the three unité paysagère tables that name Cubique, and each bullet says which. The Code gives no storey count for the style itself, no roof pitch in degrees, no window type, no lot or setback dimension in metres, no roofing material and no garage rule, so those cells are empty; the two storeys come from the unit tables. The phase is provisional: the Code groups this style with the Boomtown as one of the two that rebuilt the village after the fire, which it dates 1900 twice and 1901 once, but it gives no span for the Cubique itself.
