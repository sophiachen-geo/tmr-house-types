Two to three storeys under a straight two-slope or hipped tin roof, faced in brick or cut stone at the front and clapboard down the sides — a distinction of expense that the fiche records plainly. Large-paned windows in strict symmetry, and pediments, cornices and pilasters at the door.

Where it comes from

The idiom of the Lévis bourgeoisie in the decades around incorporation in 1861. Its public monument is the église Notre-Dame-de-la-Victoire of 1851 on plans by Thomas Baillargé, which the City's walking-tour brochure calls « un chef-d'œuvre néoclassique » and reads through its four Doric pilasters carrying a pediment.

What Ville de Lévis says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • The City counts about 50 buildings of this current across the merged territory of Lévis (fiche, « Nombre à Lévis »).
  • Documented in Vieux-Lévis around the carré Déziel by the City's walking-tour brochure Le Vieux-Lévis à pied.
Massing
  • Two to three storeys.
  • Rectangular plan.
  • Straight two-slope or hipped roof in standing-seam tin.
  • Chimneys; gables that observe the symmetry.
  • 2 à 3 étages
  • cheminées, pignons respectant la symétrie
  • rectangulaire
  • à 2 versants droits ou à croupe, tôle à baguettes
Articulation
  • Window and door surrounds, pediments, doorcases, cornices, pilasters.
  • chambranles, frontons, portails, corniches, pilastres
Openings
  • Sash or casement windows with large panes, symmetrically arranged; gabled or hipped dormers.
  • fenêtres à guillotine ou à battants à grands carreaux disposées symétriquement, lucarnes à pignon ou à croupe
Materials
  • Brick or cut stone on the principal façade, wood clapboard on the secondary elevations.
  • brique ou pierre de taille en façade, clin de bois sur façades secondaires
