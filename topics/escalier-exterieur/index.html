L'escalier extérieur — the outside stair

The stair climbing the front of a Montréal plex is the most recognisable thing about the city's housing, and almost everything commonly said about why it exists is wrong. It is not thrift, not climate, and not a fire regulation. It is a by-product of a setback rule: once buildings had to stand back from the street, the stair that used to be inside moved out into the space the rule created. This page holds the explanation once, so that every plex type record can point at it — and holds, equally, the one date this site refuses to publish.

The mechanism, in the study's own words

Patri-Arch's Étude typomorphologique de l'arrondissement du Sud-Ouest (2005, updated October 2013) states the causal chain in a single sentence, in its synthesis of the 1875–1945 period of municipal infrastructure. Construction regulation begins in Montréal around 1880; it produces a new mode of subdivision, with simple lanes, then I-shaped, then H-shaped ones, giving blocks a regular parcellation. And then:

De plus, l'obligation de l'implantation avec une marge de recul avant amènera la translation de l'escalier d'accès aux logements des étages supérieurs vers l'extérieur, ce qui donnera la caractéristique si typique des bâtiments des quartiers montréalais de cette époque.
  • The rule is about implantation, not about stairs. A compulsory front setback took buildable depth off a lot that was already narrow. Moving the stair to the upper dwellings outside recovered that depth as interior floor area, in a strip of ground the by-law had just made unbuildable anyway.
  • The second gain is thermal and it follows from the first: no interior stair means no shared hall to heat, and no shared hall means each dwelling keeps its full plan. This is the consequence, not the cause. The popular explanation reverses them.
  • The exterior stair is therefore inseparable from the front setback and from the plex plan. Where the setback was not required — the older faubourg fabric built flush to the street — the stair stayed inside.

The sequence

The same study dates the transition and the types that carry it. The older practice did not stop at once: « La construction des lotissements sans ruelle ainsi que des bâtiments implantés sans marge de recul ne sera abandonnée que vers la fin des années 1880. » So the early years of the period still produce three-storey duplexes (type 2.4) and duplexes and triplexes with interior stairs (types 2.1 and 2.5) — the exterior stair is not yet available, because the setback that creates it is not yet general.

Then, verbatim: « Le triplex avec escalier extérieur (type architectural 2.6) fait son apparition principalement à partir de 1900. Il sera accompagné peu après de plusieurs variantes : la première est un duplex avec escalier extérieur (type architectural 2.2) et la deuxième un bâtiment de 3 étages mais à plus de 3 logements ou multiplex (type architectural 2.7). » The triplex comes first and the duplex is derived from it — the reverse of the order one would guess from dwelling counts.

Prohibited 1940 — and no repeal year is published here

This site encodes prohibited: 1940 and repealed: null, and the null is deliberate. The prohibition of new exterior stairs around 1940 is repeated consistently. The date it ended is not: three different years circulate, in three different secondary sources, none of them municipal, and none citing a by-law number. Publishing any one of them would manufacture a precision the record does not have.

A fourth chronology complicates even the 1940 date, and is recorded here rather than suppressed: the same source that gives 1980 also dates the prohibition differently, to by-laws adopted « quartier par quartier, à partir de décembre 1935 » with a city-wide ban « en 1955 ». That is a different account of both ends of the story from a single secondary source, and it is why the 1940 date is carried here as the conventional one rather than as a verified one.

  • The 1994 claim and the 1980 claim are not necessarily rivals — a general lifting and a later, conditional re-permission could both be true, in that order. But nothing found on this pass establishes either.

Why they are being built again

The exterior stair is not merely tolerated today; it is expressly provided for, and the provision is shaped to exactly the building this page is about. Article 9.8.5.1 of the Code de construction du Québec, chapitre I – Bâtiment, permits a turning or helical stair to serve as a dwelling's issue on four conditions, one of which is a ceiling on how many dwellings it may serve:

Un escalier tournant ou hélicoïdal peut servir d'issue à un logement si : le logement a un autre moyen d'évacuation ; l'escalier ne dessert pas plus que 2 logements par étage ; l'escalier a une largeur libre entre 760 mm et 860 mm ; comporte des girons égaux d'au moins 225 mm lorsque mesurés à 500 mm de l'extrémité la plus étroite ; et a une rotation dans un seul sens entre les 2 étages.
  • Two dwellings per storey is the hinge. It is the plex's own dimension: a duplex or triplex on a narrow lot puts one or two doors on each level, and the curved stair that reaches them is inside the rule. A larger building is not.
  • Article 9.8.3.1, which sets riser, tread and run dimensions, classes « les escaliers extérieurs ne desservant qu'un seul logement » with interior dwelling stairs as privé — the more permissive of the three dimension sets — rather than as public stairs. The Code treats the outside stair of a small house as a domestic stair that happens to be outdoors.
  • The Ville's current permit guidance (montreal.ca, Construire ou remplacer un escalier extérieur, updated 3 February 2026) is consistent with this and adds the municipal layer: a permit is required to build, alter or replace an exterior stair; it must generally stand at least 1.2 m from a property line; and « des matériaux non combustibles peuvent être requis dans un bâtiment de plus de 2 logements » — the same two-dwelling threshold, from the other direction.
  • Verification note. The research memo behind this part cited these provisions as arts. 29.2 / 9.8.3.1. Article 9.8.3.1 is right. The substantive rule about curved stairs and the two-dwelling ceiling is at 9.8.5.1(2), quoted above from the Régie du bâtiment du Québec's own presentation of the chapter; no article numbered 29.2 carrying this rule could be confirmed, and it is not cited here.

Which types this explains

Every type record on this site whose form depends on the outside stair should point here rather than repeat the argument. The distinction the stair draws is also what separates the plex family from what comes after it: once a building has one shared hall serving six to twelve dwellings instead of a door per dwelling, the stair goes back inside and the type changes name.

  • Plex family — the group, two to six stacked dwellings on a narrow lot.
  • Triplex with exterior stair — Sud-Ouest 2.6, the archetype, from 1900.
  • Duplex with exterior stair — Sud-Ouest 2.2, derived from the triplex.
  • Multiplex of four to six dwellings — Sud-Ouest 2.7.
  • Duplex and triplex with interior stair — Sud-Ouest 2.1 and 2.5, the pre-setback forms, and the control case: same dwelling counts, no setback, stair stays in.
  • Greystone triplex with exterior stair — the same arrangement in Montréal greystone.
  • Maison d'appartements / conciergerie — where it stops, and why: « un hall d'entrée commun qui dessert de six à douze logements ».
