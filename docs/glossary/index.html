Vocabulary

Glossary

Terms used by the by-laws, inventories and studies this site draws on, and by the site itself. French terms are shown in italics.

articulation · découpage
The by-law's word for how a façade is broken into base, body and crown, and how volumes are read against each other.
larmier
The flared drip edge of a Québec roof, extended so its overhang shelters the front door; typical of the maison canadienne.
chamois
Buff-coloured clay brick, characteristic of postwar bungalows and cottages.
cottage
Three different things on this site, and the OQLF's is only one of them. In Québec French generally, and in the OQLF's definition, a cottage is any two-storey detached house as opposed to the one-storey bungalow. Ville de Québec's thésaurus uses the word twice and means neither of those: the cottage Regency (tid 205) is a low hipped-roofed villégiature house whose eaves cover a veranda, and the cottage vernaculaire industriel (tid 505) is the standard workers' house of the industrial era. Saint-Lambert's King Cottage is a fourth thing again, named for its street.
marquise
A flat canopy over an entrance door, without posts; standard on postwar cottages and modernist apartment blocks.
faubourg
One of a city's nineteenth-century inner suburbs, outside the old walls or the old core; by extension, the plain housing vernacular that grew there.
maison de faubourg
One phrase, three distinct objects, all now on this site. Ville de Québec's maison de faubourg (thésaurus tid 303) is a rudimentary house of one to one and a half storeys under a steep two-slope roof, with three openings on the front and the door at one end — and its flat-roofed successor (tid 501) is a party-wall building of flats filling the lot. Le Plateau-Mont-Royal's faubourg family is Montréal's brick vernacular of the same century, on a different plan. Mount Royal's maison faubourienne is a 1940s by-law type that quotes that vernacular in a garden suburb, two generations later. Read the canonical form, not the phrase.
PIIA · plan d'implantation et d'intégration architecturale
A discretionary site-planning and architectural-integration programme under Québec's planning act (LAU ss. 145.15–145.20.1); it judges a project's appearance and integration, not whether it is warranted.
plex
Superposed dwellings under one roof, each with its own entrance. Montréal's plex is defined by the exterior stair climbing to the upper door and by a ground floor raised above the street; Québec City's (thésaurus tid 503) allows the stair to be interior and does not require the raised ground floor. Outremont's duplexes are paired side to side rather than stacked with a stair. Same word, three arrangements — the canonical form plex-superposed-units holds what they share.
courant · courant architectural
A municipality's own word for a category of building. Ville de Québec says courant and puts its types under nine of them; Ville de Lévis says style for objects at the same level; Ville de Gatineau says type architectural. On this site the shared vocabulary is deliberately split in two — canonical form for the shape of the building, style for the idiom it is dressed in — and each place's native word is recorded in its typology document's format field.
unité de paysage
A landscape unit: a stretch of territory read as one coherent piece for planning purposes, by its street pattern, topography, setbacks, planting and building character together, rather than by building type. Outremont's 2020 study cuts the borough into four aires and twenty-nine of them; the Vieux-Terrebonne declaration of 2026 uses four.
prise d'effet
The date a Québec heritage protection begins to bite, which is the date of the minister's notice of intent — not the date of the decree that follows it. Vieux-Terrebonne was declared on 20 May 2026 with effect from 2 July 2025; that is the statute working normally, not a clerical error.
site patrimonial déclaré
Territory protected by the government of Québec under the Loi sur le patrimoine culturel — the strongest area-based status in the province, and distinct from a site patrimonial cité, which a municipality declares over its own territory. Québec City has four declared sites; Gatineau has cited ones; Lévis has neither over its historic cores.
