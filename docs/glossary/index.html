Vocabulary

Glossary

Terms used by the by-laws, inventories and studies this site draws on, and by the site itself. French terms are shown in italics.

articulation · découpage
The by-law's word for how a façade is broken into base, body and crown, and how volumes are read against each other.
larmier
The flared drip edge of a Québec roof, extended so its overhang shelters the front door; typical of the maison canadienne.
chamois
Buff-coloured clay brick, characteristic of postwar bungalows and cottages.
cottage
Three different things on this site, and the OQLF's is only one of them. In Québec French generally, and in the OQLF's definition, a cottage is any two-storey detached house as opposed to the one-storey bungalow. Ville de Québec's thésaurus uses the word twice and means neither of those: the cottage Regency (tid 205) is a low hipped-roofed villégiature house whose eaves cover a veranda, and the cottage vernaculaire industriel (tid 505) is the standard workers' house of the industrial era. Saint-Lambert's King Cottage is a fourth thing again, named for its street.
marquise
A flat canopy over an entrance door, without posts; standard on postwar cottages and modernist apartment blocks.
faubourg
One of a city's nineteenth-century inner suburbs, outside the old walls or the old core; by extension, the plain housing vernacular that grew there.
maison de faubourg
One phrase, three distinct objects, all now on this site. Ville de Québec's maison de faubourg (thésaurus tid 303) is a rudimentary house of one to one and a half storeys under a steep two-slope roof, with three openings on the front and the door at one end — and its flat-roofed successor (tid 501) is a party-wall building of flats filling the lot. Le Plateau-Mont-Royal's faubourg family is Montréal's brick vernacular of the same century, on a different plan. Mount Royal's maison faubourienne is a 1940s by-law type that quotes that vernacular in a garden suburb, two generations later. Read the canonical form, not the phrase.
PIIA · plan d'implantation et d'intégration architecturale
A discretionary site-planning and architectural-integration programme under Québec's planning act (LAU ss. 145.15–145.20.1); it judges a project's appearance and integration, not whether it is warranted.
plex
Superposed dwellings under one roof, each with its own street entrance. The exterior stair is the popular image of the Montréal plex but it is not the definition: the Sud-Ouest typology defines its family 2 by dwelling count alone — « Ces immeubles possèdent de deux à six logements » — and files the interior-stair duplex (2.1) and interior-stair triplex (2.5) in that family beside the exterior-stair ones. Québec City's (thésaurus tid 503) likewise allows an interior stair and does not require the raised ground floor. Outremont's duplexes are paired side to side rather than stacked with a stair. Villeray–Saint-Michel–Parc-Extension stretches the word furthest, filing its one-storey single-family shoebox under the plex heading. Same word, several arrangements — the canonical group plex-family holds what they share, and plex-superposed-units is an alias of it.
courant · courant architectural
A municipality's own word for a category of building. Ville de Québec says courant and puts its types under nine of them; Ville de Lévis says style for objects at the same level; Ville de Gatineau says type architectural. On this site the shared vocabulary is deliberately split in two — canonical form for the shape of the building, style for the idiom it is dressed in — and each place's native word is recorded in its typology document's format field.
unité de paysage
A landscape unit: a stretch of territory read as one coherent piece for planning purposes, by its street pattern, topography, setbacks, planting and building character together, rather than by building type. Outremont's 2020 study cuts the borough into four aires and twenty-nine of them; the Vieux-Terrebonne declaration of 2026 uses four.
prise d'effet
The date a Québec heritage protection begins to bite, which is the date of the minister's notice of intent — not the date of the decree that follows it. Vieux-Terrebonne was declared on 20 May 2026 with effect from 2 July 2025; that is the statute working normally, not a clerical error.
site patrimonial déclaré
Territory protected by the government of Québec under the Loi sur le patrimoine culturel — the strongest area-based status in the province, and distinct from a site patrimonial cité, which a municipality declares over its own territory. Québec City has four declared sites; Gatineau has cited ones; Lévis has neither over its historic cores.
shoebox · maison de type « shoebox »
A one-storey, flat-roofed, small urban house on a rectangular plan set end-on to the street, its door at the centre of the façade between two symmetrical windows, usually in brick with a cornice or parapet as its crown, and often a front galerie under a marquise. The name is about the box, not the shoe: Villeray–Saint-Michel–Parc-Extension's 2019 guide says the appellation refers to a rectangular volume evoking a shoebox. Two things make it unlike anything else on this site. It was built to be built on: the flat roof is there « pour permettre de futures expansions architecturales », so the finished house is the first storey of an unfinished one. And the earliest generations were self-built — « des constructions modestes réalisées à faible coût par leurs propriétaires à partir des matériaux disponibles » — which no other type here can claim. It appears at the start of the twentieth century and is built in phases down to the 1960s. Rosemont–La Petite-Patrie treats it as one type; Villeray–Saint-Michel–Parc-Extension splits it in two, an early-twentieth-century shoebox and a mid-century one that borrows bungalow traits. That is a real typological disagreement between two boroughs of one city, not a difference of wording.
uniplex
A coined name for the single-dwelling members of the plex family — a plex-shaped building with one dwelling in it, sometimes with a second in the basement. It is not standard Québec usage: the conventional series starts at duplex for two dwellings, and a one-dwelling building is an habitation unifamiliale. It is recorded here because the research behind this part reports Villeray–Saint-Michel–Parc-Extension proposing it for its shoebox family, and because the site's canonical id for that form carries it. Treat it as a proposal, not as a term of art: the borough's own published typology, règlement 01-283-124, Annexe F, files these buildings under 1.0 Maisons unifamiliales and does not use the word.
maison de ville
A false friend, and one of the sharpest on this site. The English town house is an old party-wall house on a narrow deep lot in a dense street — what Québec sources would call a maison en rangée or a maison urbaine. The Sud-Ouest's maison de ville, type 1.5 of the Patri-Arch typo-morphological study, is almost its opposite: a two-storey single-family house under a pitched gable or hipped roof, semi-detached or attached, built from the 1960s and 1970s onward out of redevelopment projects in La Petite-Bourgogne and under Opération 20 000 logements. It sits behind a front setback of five to eight metres with hedges, trees and a parking space, often has an internal garage reached by a ramp, wears asphalt shingle, and has a sober façade with almost no ornament. The study says plainly that these ensembles are « en rupture avec les développements urbains traditionnels ». So the phrase names a suburban product inserted into an old fabric, not the old fabric itself. Read the type number, not the words.
Verdun
A disambiguation rather than a building term, and worth one line because the two are easy to collide in a search. Verdun here is the Montréal borough — a separate city from 1907 to 2002, third-largest in Québec in the 1920s — whose heritage sectors are coded 21.E.n in the Ville de Montréal's Évaluation du patrimoine urbain. Verdun in the Meuse, in France, is a different place with a heritage instrument of its own: a site patrimonial remarquable classed by arrêté of 13 April 2022. Nothing on this site refers to the French commune.
