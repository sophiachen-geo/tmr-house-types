Residential Architectural Styles of Trois-Rivières

Trois-Rivières was founded on 4 July 1634, when Laviolette raised a fort at the mouth of the Saint-Maurice at Champlain's request, and it is the second oldest city in New France. Menaced by the Iroquois, the post became a walled bourg in 1650; under Pierre Boucher the inhabitants built their houses inside the palisade, and the line of that palisade, together with Jacques Levasseur de Neré's palisade project of 1704, still fixes the boundary of the declared heritage site. Rue des Ursulines runs the length of it, five streets traced from 1650 cross it, and the grid they make is the oldest thing on the ground.

The city that grew outward from that grid was made twice. The first making was industrial: sawmills at the mouth of the Saint-Maurice from 1854, the railway in the 1870s, and then the pulp and paper mills that earned Trois-Rivières the title of capitale mondiale du papier journal. The second was catastrophic. On 22 June 1908 a fire took the commercial centre; the reconstruction that followed was carried out inside four years by, in the City's phrase, une poignée d'architectes et d'entrepreneurs, and the quarter they left has a uniformity that is still legible. That uniformity is not incidental to the heritage claim — it is the heritage claim. A centre rebuilt at one moment, in one material, to one profile, is a rarer thing in Québec than a centre of mixed date.

What is easy to get wrong, and what the ministry's own documents make plain, is where the fire fell. The declared site was peu affecté. Inside it stand the Ursulines' conventual chapel of 1714–1716, the Récollets' presbytery of 1742 and their chapel of 1754 — now the anglican église Saint-James — a wooden house of about 1757, and three classed stone houses. Around it, and pressing on it from the south-west, is the rebuilt city: the widths of rues des Casernes and des Ursulines are themselves post-fire works, and thirteen of the site's forty-five buildings, close to a third, went up between 1908 and 1925.

1634–1760 · The French-regime bourg

1634 – 1760

The lieu was frequented by Indigenous people long before Europeans arrived, and served as a meeting place between them and the fur traders; Jacques Cartier stopped in 1535 and François Gravé Du Pont came about 1599. The post was founded in 1634, a habitation built and a Jesuit mission established; the bourg was fortified in 1650. In 1663 Trois-Rivières was made the seat of a regional gouvernement, the equal of Québec and Montréal — the City's own history page dates this to 1665, one of several small disagreements between the sources noted below. The Ursulines arrived in 1697, moved into their monastery in 1700, and about 1714 raised a chapel and a small hospital. Part of the bourg burned in 1752 and the Ursulines' buildings burned with it; rebuilt the following year, they burned again in 1806. Growth was slow, checked by Montréal's rise as the hinge of the fur trade. Of the domestic architecture of this whole period only about a dozen buildings survive anywhere in the city, and the survivors that matter are concentrated in a few hundred metres of rue des Ursulines.

The house the first colonists built and the notables rebuilt in stone: a squat rectangle sitting straight on the ground without a cellar, one floor under a steep roof that could reach 55 degrees, few windows because the winter demanded few, and massive chimneys because it demanded many. In town the party walls run up past the roof as fire-breaks. Trois-Rivières had almost no local stone, so the stone version was always the exception — the mark of a family with the means to ship it from the south shore.

Where it comes from

Brought from rural north-western France — Brittany, Normandy, the Île-de-France — and adapted fast to a country with different materials and a harsher winter. The RPCQ says of the declared site that "le Régime français a légué… la maison Saint-François… des résidences de notables en pierre, comme le manoir de Tonnancour (1795-1797)". The form outlived the Conquest by the isolation of the population, and buildings put up between 1780 and 1820 still show it; from the 1820s, crossed with English neoclassicism, it produced the maison de tradition québécoise. The 1908 fire destroyed several of the city's French-regime buildings, including the parish church and the maison des Gouverneurs.

Historical context (fiche)
  • Les premières constructions érigées au moment de la fondation de Trois-Rivières sont des constructions modestes élevées à la hâte en pleine nature sauvage. Originaires des milieux ruraux de France, les colons ainsi que les gens de métier – charpentiers, maçons, menuisiers – apportent au pays leur savoir-faire traditionnel en matière de construction.
  • Cette première architecture est issue des traditions constructives de la France, tout particulièrement des régions du nord-ouest telles que la Bretagne, la Normandie et l'Île-de-France.
  • La pierre est quasi absente de la région et doit être apportée de la rive sud. Ainsi, les rares habitations de pierre sur le territoire trifluvien appartenaient à des familles bien nanties ou d'une certaine notoriété. La pierre est souvent réservée aux bâtiments plus imposants tels les moulins, les couvents et les églises.
  • Comme en témoignent certains bâtiments construits entre 1780 et 1820, ce courant perdure au-delà de la Conquête anglaise en raison de l'isolement de la population de la Nouvelle-France. Continuant à évoluer et subissant l'influence de l'architecture anglaise, la maison coloniale française engendre, à partir des années 1820, la maison dite de tradition québécoise.
Siting & landscape
  • Built at ground level, without a cellar, on shallow stone footings; the house is entered from the flat.
  • In town, set with the façade directly on the street, so that the party walls carried above the roof do the work a setback would otherwise do.
  • Stone was almost absent from the Trois-Rivières district and had to be brought from the south shore, so the rare stone houses belonged to the well-off or the notable; stone was otherwise reserved for mills, convents and churches.

Conservation guidance (fiche)
  • Prefer: preserve the imposing volume and the squat aspect of these houses, and the asymmetrical distribution of the openings, which should also stay few in number.
  • Prefer: use traditional materials — stone and wood for the walls, wood shingle or traditional tin for the roofs, and wood for doors and windows. Avoid modern materials such as vinyl and aluminium.
  • Avoid: adding ornament. Simplicity and bareness are what characterise these buildings.
  • Avoid: moving, enlarging, adding or altering doors, windows and dormers.
  • Avoid: uprooting old buildings from the place they were built. The City's own fiche makes the point about the 1781 windmill, moved to the university grounds in 1974: the move saved it from demolition but cost it part of its historical meaning.
  • Lors de travaux de restauration ou de rénovations, il est impératif de préserver le volume imposant et l'aspect trapu de ces demeures, ainsi que la distribution asymétrique des ouvertures qui doivent d'ailleurs demeurer peu nombreuses.
  • Idéalement, des matériaux traditionnels devraient être employés : pierre et bois pour les façades, bardeau de bois ou tôle traditionnelle pour les toits et ouvertures en bois. L'utilisation de matériaux modernes, tels que le vinyle et l'aluminium, est à éviter.
  • Il faut finalement résister à l'idée d'ajouter des ornements, car la simplicité et le dépouillement caractérisent ces bâtiments.
  • Il vaut mieux éviter de déplacer, d'agrandir, d'ajouter ou de modifier des portes, des fenêtres et des lucarnes.
  • Il faut éviter de déraciner des bâtiments anciens de leur lieu d'origine.

1760–1860 · British regime

1760 – 1860

After the Conquest the government of Trois-Rivières was abolished, and the town held its standing by other means: seat of a judicial district — the RPCQ's own fiche says 1792 in one section and 1793 in another — and seat of a bishopric in 1852. The Americans occupied the town briefly in 1776. Neoclassicism arrived with the British military engineers and the Scottish and English architects, and it took hold here more slowly and lasted longer than elsewhere: the City's fiche remarks that in Trois-Rivières le néoclassicisme cédera plus lentement qu'ailleurs la place à l'éclectisme. Its domestic form is the georgian house — two and a half storeys, symmetrical, brick, standing alone in a large lot, with cornice returns beginning a pediment. The maison Hertel-De La Fresnière of 1824 belongs to the seam between the two regimes: French in its siting on the ground, its steep roof and its fire-break walls, English in the symmetry of its openings and chimneys and in its carved window surrounds.

1860–1908 · Forestry capital of the Mauricie

1860 – 1908

Industrialisation in the second half of the nineteenth century made Trois-Rivières the centre of the Mauricie forest industry. Norcross and Philips built a sawmill at the mouth of the Saint-Maurice in 1854; the cité was constituted in 1857 and divided into four quarters, the old sector falling into the quartier Sainte-Ursule; the rail link came in the 1870s and the port was modernised. A new business bourgeoisie and a growing working population arrived together, and the housing divided accordingly. On the terrasse Turcotte and along rue des Ursulines the merchants built in stone and brick with copper roofs and elaborate trim; in the faubourgs, from the 1880s, the mansard house let workers inhabit the attic, and after it came the Boomtown house — balloon-framed, two storeys, flat or barely sloping to the rear, its ornament gathered in a cornice or a parapet at the top of the front. Trois-Rivières has enough of them, the City's fiche says, pour la faire apparaître comme caractéristique de la ville.

1908–1920 · The fire and the reconstruction

1908 – 1920

The fire of 22 June 1908 began in a hay store and ran through the centre; the City's brigade of eight firefighters was pushed back within a quarter of an hour and help was called from Shawinigan, Grand-Mère, Québec and Montréal. How much burned depends entirely on whom you read, and the figures are set out with their attributions in the notes below. What is not in dispute is the shape of the loss — the whole quarter between the river and rues Bonaventure, Champlain and Saint-Georges, with the parish church and the maison des Gouverneurs among the buildings destroyed, several of them French-regime — and the shape of the recovery. By 1912 the centre was busy again. The streets were widened and in places redrawn, the city expanded west and north, and the new fabric was brick: a city still mostly of wood in 1900 was 55 % brick-housed by 1931. The Second Empire houses at 186-190 rue Bonaventure are post-fire; so are the wider carriageways of rues des Casernes and des Ursulines; so is the Appartements Laviolette of 1918, which replaced a building the flames had probably taken.

1907–1945 · Pulp and paper — the workers' parishes

1907 – 1945

The mills came in on the heels of the fire and did more to shape the city than the fire did. The Wabasso Cotton was built in 1907 on rue Saint-Paul; the Wayagamack Pulp and Paper and the St. Lawrence Pulp and Paper followed on the Saint-Maurice, and the Canadian International Paper mill of the 1920s was for a time the largest papermaking plant in the world. New parishes were erected to hold the workers — Saint-Philippe in 1909, Notre-Dame-des-Sept-Allégresses in 1911, Sainte-Cécile in 1912 — and each brought a church, a presbytery and a school with it. Between 1910 and 1940 the population tripled while the built-up territory only doubled. With no urban plan and heavy land speculation, the houses came closer together and then shared walls; large flats of seven and eight rooms were subdivided; stables, henhouses and sheds were converted to dwellings. The Boomtown house of the 1890s and 1900s gave way, from about 1910 and decisively in the 1920s, to the plex: two or three storeys of superposed flats, each with its own door reached by its own stair, under the flat roof that coal tar had made possible.

Where it comes from

The form is one of the strongest comparisons this site can make, because six independent municipalities describe it in their own words and the descriptions agree. Immeuble à logements — the plex of superposed flats L'immeuble à logements / l'immeuble de type « plex »

Style: Vernaculaire industriel (Québec industrial vernacular) / Boomtown (false front)
Courant: Immeuble de type « plex »
QOSP-TR
source ›
Phase attribution: verified

When coal tar made the waterproof flat roof possible, the urban dwelling became a stack. Two or three storeys of superposed flats, each with its own door off the street reached by its own stair, put up at speed on cramped lots by the first property developers the city had seen, to house the mill workers. The oldest of them still carry the Boomtown's gallery-and-balcony arrangement — the fiche calls that their filiation — while the parapet turns from an applied cornice into stepped brick with friezes, and then into curves and counter-curves.

Where it comes from

The plex coexisted with the Boomtown house and then supplanted it about 1910; it is the house that matches the accelerated urban growth of 1910 to 1930, and by the 1920s the Patri-Arch Synthèse records it as the principal workers' dwelling in Trois-Rivières. The Depression and the Second World War left only an impoverished version, and it faded slowly in favour of the apartment block with a single common entrance. The interwar plex is a near relation of proto-rationalist architecture — the regular-framed, plainly detailed brick manufactories and warehouses of 1850-1890 — and it is the rational, simple, easily built and economical side of it that ties the plex to that current. The same superposed-dwelling form is followed through the canonical layer under `plex-superposed-units`.

A naming caution the sources force. The Ville de Trois-Rivières catalogues TWO distinct currents here, and this record encodes the first — "Immeuble de type « plex »", where every dwelling has its own direct entrance from the street — and not the second, "Immeuble d'appartements", which the same fiche distinguishes explicitly: "Contrairement aux bâtiments de type « plex », les immeubles d'appartements possèdent une seule et même entrée commune à tous les logements." The RPCQ and the Plan de conservation, writing about the declared site, use the phrase "immeubles à logements" for buildings whose described characteristics — cubic volume, flat roof, two to three storeys, ornamented cornice, wood or wrought-iron balconies — match the plex. The record slug follows the ministerial phrase and the French name carries both, so that neither vocabulary is lost. The apartment block with one common entrance is a separate form and is not encoded here.

Cubic house (Four-square) La maison cubique (courant cubique)

Style: American Foursquare / Vernaculaire industriel (Québec industrial vernacular)
Courant: Cubique
source ›
Phase attribution: verified

Two full storeys on a square plan under a shallow hipped roof — Frank Kidder's Four-square of 1891, sold through American plan catalogues on the strength of how much room it gave for the money, and taken up across Québec in the first half of the twentieth century. In Trois-Rivières it settled equally on the bare rural plains and in the denser village cores, and its ornament is a straightforward index of the first owner's means: chambranles only on one house, corner boards, turned columns and worked railings on the next.

Where it comes from

One branch of the industrial vernacular that standardised materials, mechanised the work and distributed plans through catalogues and magazines from the end of the nineteenth century, alongside the balloon frame that made building fast. The same square hipped block is followed through the canonical layer under `foursquare-hipped-2st`, where it can be set beside the versions catalogued by Saint-Lambert, Gatineau, Québec City and Lévis — a form that arrived from the United States by catalogue and was then renamed locally in every place it landed.

Roofs of unequal slope running into one another, big chimneys, exposed rafters under wide overhangs, cedar shingle and stucco and stone used together, and windows put wherever the plan wanted them. The City's fiche declines to give a type description and says so: every case is unique and has its own characteristics. In Trois-Rivières it arrived with the paper company — place Freeman, the old terrasse Saint-Maurice, was the address of the Saint-Maurice Paper Company's bosses — and then filtered down through every layer of society until 1939.

Where it comes from

Ruskin and Morris, reacting against industrialisation and standardisation by way of English craft and the English country house; the movement converged with the garden city. Crossing to the United States it split into the Shingle Style of the east coast, the Prairie Style of the centre and the Craftsman of the west, and the catalogues and plan magazines carried all three across North America through the first half of the twentieth century. The City's fiche is candid that the idea did not survive the crossing — "en Amérique, le mouvement Arts & Crafts devient essentiellement un mouvement stylistique, sans portée sociale."

Historical context (fiche)
  • Au tournant du 19e siècle, en Angleterre, la société traditionnelle subit des transformations majeures en raison de l'industrialisation. C'est alors qu'apparaissent des mouvements de pensée en réaction contre cette perte de la structure sociale traditionnelle, des mœurs, de la religion et de la ville. Ce mouvement et celui de la cité-jardin, véritable révolution urbanistique de la fin du 19e siècle, convergent. On veut remplacer les quartiers d'habitation ouvrière sordides par de nouveaux, planifiés selon des principes humanistes.
  • Pour apprécier l'architecture du courant Arts & Crafts, il faut remonter aux théories du critique d'art John Ruskin (1819-1900) et de l'artiste et écrivain William Morris (1834-1896), tous deux Britanniques. Ils proposent de s'inspirer de l'artisanat et de l'habitation de la campagne anglaise afin de créer une architecture qui s'inscrive dans la tradition nationale. Le retour au travail manuel, l'usage de matériaux traditionnels ainsi que le recours aux savoir-faire locaux sont valorisés pour faire obstacle à la standardisation.
  • Si les réalisations du mouvement sont largement diffusées, son idéologie demeure mal comprise. Ainsi, en Amérique, le mouvement Arts & Crafts devient essentiellement un mouvement stylistique, sans portée sociale. D'abord adopté par l'élite anglophone, il n'est pas surprenant de retrouver quelques exemples de ce style pour les résidences de dirigeants de grande compagnie, comme c'est le cas à la place Freeman (ancienne terrasse Saint-Maurice) qui était réservée aux patrons de la Saint-Maurice Paper Company. Ensuite, cette architecture a eu une influence dans toutes les couches de la société jusqu'à la Seconde Guerre mondiale.
Siting & landscape
  • First taken up by the anglophone elite, so unsurprisingly found among the residences of the directors of large companies — as at the place Freeman, formerly terrasse Saint-Maurice, which was reserved for the bosses of the Saint-Maurice Paper Company. Thereafter the architecture influenced every layer of society until the Second World War.
  • Where several stand together they can form ensembles whose forms marry to one another, avoiding the repetition of a single base model.
Massing
  • Generally imposing volume on an articulated plan; the volume, however modest, is dynamically articulated.
  • Roofs of varied form with slopes of unequal length, and prominent chimneys.
  • Sheltered exterior spaces — perrons, galleries, terraces.
  • Coquettish little houses with roofs of unequal pitch, important chimneys and varied fenestration piercing walls of brick, stucco or cedar shingle.
  • Volume généralement imposant au plan articulé
  • En général, l'architecture du mouvement Arts & Crafts est exclusivement réservée à la construction résidentielle. Ces constructions prennent l'allure de coquettes maisonnettes aux toits à pentes inégales, aux cheminées importantes, à la fenestration variée perçant les murs de brique, de stuc ou de bardeau de cèdre. Le volume de ces maisons, malgré sa modestie, n'en est pas moins articulé de façon dynamique. Lorsqu'elles sont regroupées, ces maisons peuvent constituer des ensembles aux formes se mariant les unes aux autres, évitant la répétition d'un modèle de base.
  • Espaces extérieurs protégés (perrons, galeries, terrasses)
  • Toitures de formes variées à versants de longueurs inégales, présence de cheminées
Articulation
  • Ornament often limited to the elements of the frame — half-timbering, exposed rafters.
  • Every case is unique and has its own characteristics.
  • Cedar shingle used in the railings of gallery-porches, or as cladding, is a second distinguishing element to keep.
  • Ornements souvent limités aux éléments de la charpente (colombages, chevrons apparents)
  • Chaque cas est unique et possède ses propres caractéristiques
Openings
  • Numerous and diverse openings, with paired windows and dormers.
  • Ouvertures nombreuses et diversifiées, présence de fenêtres jumelées et de lucarnes
Materials
  • Natural and traditional materials — stone, brick, stucco, cedar shingle, wood boards — sometimes combined with one another.
  • Utilisation de matériaux naturels et traditionnels (pierre, brique, stuc, bardeau de cèdre, planches de bois), parfois combinés entre eux
Sous-variantes
  • Les résidences ainsi érigées peuvent prendre des formes diverses selon qu'elles se rapprochent davantage d'une des principales variantes. Celles s'inspirant du « Stick Style » se démarquent par la présence des structures à colombages et des chevrons et consoles soutenant la toiture.
  • Le « Shingle Style », de son côté, se distingue par ses toits enveloppants, souvent à croupes ou à demi-croupe, percés de lucarnes, ses murs généralement revêtus de bardeau de bois, ses nombreuses fenêtres et ses espaces extérieurs protégés.
  • Le bungalow « Craftsman » propose un volume compact, d'un étage et demi avec toit à deux versants se prolongeant au-dessus d'une importante galerie. Les maisons de style Arts & Crafts présentent des plans bien articulés, des toitures imposantes aux versants inégaux laissant voir les chevrons et des fenêtres nombreuses et variées.

Encoded as a seventh record because the City's own material warrants it. The brief that commissioned this place quoted a single caption — "Résidence Arts & Crafts reconnaissable par ses jeux de toitures et son revêtement de bardeau de cèdre. 11, place Freeman" — and made the record conditional on the page giving more. It gives a great deal more: seven "Caractéristiques architecturales du courant" bullets, four paragraphs of historique, a Variantes section distinguishing Stick Style, Shingle Style and the Craftsman bungalow, a Conservation section, and nine captioned addresses. The phase is provisional: the City's page gives no date range for this courant, and p5 is inferred from the fiche's own framing — the movement reaching every layer of society "jusqu'à la Seconde Guerre mondiale", the place Freeman houses belonging to the Saint-Maurice Paper Company era, and the catalogues popularising the tendencies "durant toute la première moitié du 20e siècle". Not characterised in the declared-site texts, so `sectors` is empty. The canonical form is `complex-gable-roof-house`, chosen because the interpenetrating roofs of unequal slope are what every one of the City's captions singles out; the Craftsman bungalow variant does not fit it as well, and no second canonical form has been asserted on inference.

Conservation guidance (fiche)
  • Prefer: since the Arts & Crafts spirit calls for a return to traditional materials and techniques, do not modernise these houses with contemporary industrial or synthetic materials. Wood doors and windows remain preferable to modern aluminium or PVC; the same advice applies to wall claddings.
  • Prefer: keep the expressed frame members, above all the rafters, under the wide roof overhangs. Do not box them into enamelled aluminium or vinyl on the pretext of ending repeated maintenance. Keep the wood shingle too, used in the railings of gallery-porches or as cladding.
  • Prefer: stucco cracks and repairs to it leave marks — patching stucco is next to impossible, so repairs must be concealed with a product prepared for external application on a cementitious material.
  • Avoid: since these houses are relatively recent, resist the temptation of a fake antiquity — false shutters bolted to the wall, removable false glazing bars fitted to make the glass look divided. Aluminium storm windows and awnings of polychrome sheet metal or corrugated translucent plastic are also to be banned.
  • L'esprit Arts & Crafts réclamant un retour vers les matériaux et techniques traditionnels, il serait inadéquat de moderniser ces résidences en optant pour des matériaux contemporains industrialisés ou synthétiques. Ainsi, portes et fenêtres en bois demeurent préférables aux modèles modernes en aluminium ou en PVC. Le même conseil s'applique aux revêtements muraux.
  • Un des éléments distinctifs des résidences issues de ce mouvement est l'expression des membrures de charpente, des chevrons surtout, sous les larges débords de toits. Il faut s'abstenir de les emboîter dans un enrobement d'aluminium émaillé ou de vinyle sous le prétexte de mettre fin à l'entretien répété. Autre élément distinctif à conserver : le bardeau de bois, employé dans les garde-corps de galeries-porches ou comme revêtement.
  • Se pose pour certaines d'entre elles le problème de l'entretien et de la réparation des parements de stuc. Ce matériau à composition cimentaire a tendance à produire des fissures dont la réparation laisse des traces. Il est pratiquement impossible de ragréer une réparation de stuc. Les reprises doivent donc être dissimulées avec un produit préparé pour application à l'extérieur, sur matériau cimentaire.
  • Ces maisons étant relativement récentes, il convient, lorsqu'on les rénove, de résister à la tentation de l'ancienneté factice : faux volets boulonnés au mur, faux petit bois amovible pour faciliter l'entretien du vitrage et destiné à créer l'apparence des carreaux de verre. Les contre-fenêtres en aluminium ainsi que les auvents de tôle polychrome ou de plastique translucide ondulé sont également à proscrire.

1945–2002 · Bungalow belt

1945 – 2002

After 1945 the bungalow took the ground that vernacular architecture had held, most of it built after the adoption of the 1957 urban plan, in the outer parts of the old quarters. The City's own fiche is unsentimental about it: la production rapide, peu originale et parfois peu résistante, de maisons vendues. The pont Laviolette opened in 1967. A large urban-renewal project gave the city a new hôtel de ville in 1968, a cultural centre, underground parking and the place de l'Hôtel-de-Ville, which won the Vincent Massey award in 1971. On 4 July 2001 the province decreed the merger of Trois-Rivières with Trois-Rivières-Ouest, Pointe-du-Lac, Cap-de-la-Madeleine, Sainte-Marthe-du-Cap and Saint-Louis-de-France; it took effect on 1 January 2002, and the inventory that followed in 2008–2010 covers all six.

Notes on the approach

Two scales, never to be conflated. There are two bodies of evidence here and they measure different things. The declared site is about 6.7 hectares and, depending which ministerial document you consult, 45 or about 50 buildings: the RPCQ fiche says la cinquantaine de bâtiments, the ministry's own 2014 Plan de conservation counts 45 bâtiments principaux. The city-wide inventory, carried out by Patri-Arch in 2008–2010 across all six merged municipalities, covers 3,856 buildings, of which the City reports 250 retained for exceptional and superior heritage value. Those two numbers describe different territories at different resolutions and must never be added, averaged or substituted for one another. A third figure belongs to neither: the inventory's own Introduction et méthodologie says that "environ 200 bâtiments de valeur exceptionnelle ou supérieure ou situés à l'intérieur de l'arrondissement historique" received a written énoncé de valeur patrimoniale — a count of documents produced, not of buildings retained. The type records on this page come from the city-wide courants; each names in its sectors field whether the declared-site texts also describe it inside the perimeter.

The fire's contested fraction. The sources disagree, and the disagreement is not trivial, so the figures are given here with their attributions rather than resolved. The Ville de Trois-Rivières, on its "Histoire de Trois-Rivières" page, gives no fraction and no death toll: "Le 22 juin 1908, Trois-Rivières connaît l'incendie le plus dévastateur de son histoire… Près de 800 bâtiments sont rasés, ruinant et jetant des centaines de personnes à la rue." Patri-Arch, in the Synthèse the City itself commissioned, gives a much smaller count and a wider perimeter: "L'incendie du 22 juin 1908 détruit une grande part du centre-ville. Environ 200 bâtiments… sont incendiés dans le périmètre compris entre la terrasse Turcotte et la rue du Fleuve au sud, les rues René et Saint-George à l'ouest, les rues Champlain, Hart et des Prisons au nord et les rues Saint-Louis, Notre-Dame et des Champs à l'est." Radio-Canada, in "Un feu centenaire", gives the quarter and the larger count and denies any casualty: "un incendie qui a ravagé le quart de la ville"; "Quelque 800 bâtiments avaient été rasés par le feu"; "Il n'y avait eu aucun mort ni blessé". Micheline Champoux, for the UQTR, supplies what looks like the reconciliation: "En tout, 800 bâtiments, y compris hangars et entrepôts, ont brûlé", against "Plus de 200 résidences et presque tous les commerces de la ville". On that reading the ~200 and the ~800 are the same fire counted twice — principal buildings against every structure including sheds and warehouses. The figures widely repeated in popular accounts — a third of the city, 800 houses, 180 businesses, damage of $1,500,000, and a single victim named Joseph Métivier, killed by a collapsing wall the day after — could not be found on any page of the Ville de Trois-Rivières, nor in any of the City's own commissioned studies, and are recorded here as unattributed rather than repeated as municipal. The French Wikipedia article does report one death, from a wall collapse the day after rather than from the fire itself, which is consistent with Radio-Canada's "aucun mort" only if the two are counting different things.

The homogeneity is the heritage value. The reconstruction is not valuable in spite of being uniform. The City's own account rests the claim on the uniformity: "Rebâti par une poignée d'architectes et d'entrepreneurs, le quartier présente une grande homogénéité architecturale qui se constate encore aujourd'hui." Read that alongside the Plan de conservation's numbers for the declared site — 28 of 45 buildings in brick, 18 of them after 1908; 21 of 45 under flat roofs; 39 of 45 at two, two and a half or three storeys — and the same evenness is measurable a few streets away.

About a dozen French-regime buildings survive city-wide. The stock is thin and it is concentrated. Inside the declared site, three residential witnesses of the eighteenth century remain: the maison Georges-De Gannes (about 1756), the maison Saint-François (1757) and the manoir de Tonnancour (1795–1797), with the Ursulines' chapel of 1714–1716 and the Récollets' presbytery of 1742 and church of 1754. Outside it stand the manoir Boucher-De Niverville on rue Bonaventure, the moulin seigneurial de Tonnancour on rue Notre-Dame Ouest, the old windmill of 1781 — moved to the UQTR grounds in 1974, and the City's fiche is blunt that moving it cost it part of its meaning — and the Grande Maison des Forges, entirely rebuilt on its foundations in 1988–1989. The 1908 fire took several French-regime buildings with it, including the parish church and the maison des Gouverneurs.

Disagreements inside single documents, left as found. The RPCQ fiche dates the judicial district to 1792 in its valeur patrimoniale and to 1793 in its informations historiques. The same fiche dates the seat of regional government to 1663; the City's history page says 1665. The RPCQ dates the Wayagamack to 1911, the Canadian International Paper to 1922 and the St. Lawrence Pulp and Paper to 1923, while the research memo behind this record carried 1913, 1919 and 1907/1910 for a partly different set of mills; the City's 2024 industrial study dates the Wabasso Cotton to 1907. Where a date is quoted in a type record its source is named with it.

The Boomtown attic vent is a local signature. Worth stating plainly because it is the one point on which the trifluvien version of a continent-wide form is said to differ: "La section de la façade correspondant à l'espace de l'entretoit est souvent percée de petites fenêtres de forme particulière ou losangée. Ces ouvertures, servant à l'aération ou à l'éclairage du grenier, sont une particularité de l'architecture trifluvienne que l'on retrouve peu ailleurs au Québec."
