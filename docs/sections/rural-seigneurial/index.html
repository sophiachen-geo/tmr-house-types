Section D

Rural seigneurial territories

The first places on this site whose organising unit is the farm lot rather than the street. A seigneurial territory was divided into long narrow terres running back from a river or a road, and the house sat where the survey put it — so the shape of the settlement is a land division, not a plan of blocks. Île d'Orléans strings six parishes along a single ring road around an island still 95 % agricultural; Charlesbourg's Trait-Carré radiates forty trapezoidal lots from a common, the first settlement of its kind in New France. Both are provincial sites patrimoniaux déclarés, and in both the oldest house type on this site — the low stone house of French inspiration — is still the commonest old building on the ground.
