Brick bungalow of the 1950s Mercier developments Le bungalow de brique des lotissements de Mercier

The other post-war Mercier, and the odder one. Identical brick bungalows put up in whole streets in the early 1950s — in one development with no front door at all on the street face, just a band of windows pushed to one side — laid out with rows of trees along the sidewalk that the Ville's surveyors could still recognise fifty years later as part of the original plan.

Where it comes from

Mercier is where the borough turned suburban. The Évaluation notes that the criteria it used to find heritage sectors « n'a pas permis d'identifier beaucoup de secteurs dans la partie est du territoire », and answers itself: « cela ne signifie pas pour autant que cette partie de l'arrondissement ne présente pas d'intérêt. Les qualités d'ensemble de plusieurs rues devraient être maintenues. » That is why these four developments are recorded as ensembles urbains d'intérêt rather than as heritage sectors — the value is in the coherence of a whole street, not in any single house, and the document's own recommendation is to study such territories, « recenser les typologies des ensembles qui présentent une grande homogénéité », and build public awareness of them. It is a recommendation for exactly the kind of record this page is.

What Évaluation du patrimoine urbain (2005) says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • Whole developments built to one plan, with rows of trees along the sidewalk that the cahier reads as deliberate — « les alignements d'arbres matures en bordure du trottoir, découlant d'un plan d'ensemble organisé, ajoutent à l'harmonie des rues ».
  • In the 1955 ensemble the streets run in loops, « le type de lotissement qui est popularisé dans les nouvelles banlieues développées après la guerre ».
Massing
  • One storey. In the earliest ensemble (rues Honoré-Beaugrand to Cirier, early 1950s) a square plan with no door on the principal façade at all, only a band of windows set either to the left or to the right.
  • In the 1955 ensemble (rues Honoré-Beaugrand and Chénier), detached or semi-detached, rectangular or L-plan, hipped or gabled roofs, with or without a carport.
Articulation
  • –
Openings
  • A horizontal window band as the whole of the principal façade's composition, in the earliest ensemble.
Materials
  • Brick throughout, and it stays dominant — in the 1955 ensemble most houses have taken on individuality through changed cladding or windows, « cependant, la plupart respectent leur volume et leur composition d'origine et la brique reste le matériau dominant ».

Composed from three sector fiches of the Évaluation — 27.U.2 (rues Honoré-Beaugrand to Cirier, early 1950s), 27.U.3 (rue Gustave-Bleau to place Lepailleur, early 1950s, « des bungalows en brique tous identiques ») and 27.U.5 (rues Honoré-Beaugrand and Chénier, 1955) — which describe three different developments rather than one type, and are treated here as one record because the cahier itself groups them under the same category and describes them in the same terms. Where they disagree, both readings are kept: the square doorless plan belongs to 27.U.2 only, the loop streets and the plan variety to 27.U.5 only. Articulation is left empty; none of the three fiches describes ornament. These three sectors are not in this page's sectors.yaml, which holds the five E sectors only.
