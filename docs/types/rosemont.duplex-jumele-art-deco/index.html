The plex after the car: two flats behind a brick front with a flat roof, but paired instead of contiguous, with a driveway between each pair running back to a garage at the end of the lot, and the front door under a wrought-iron marquise on chains. Built by single promoters in the 1940s and 1950s in two ensembles urbains that the cahier singles out for their uniformity.

Where it comes from

Rosemont kept growing after 1945 on the post-war demographic boom and the housing shortage, and the cahier says whole streets went up at once. These two sectors are that process caught in a form: too uniform to be the work of many builders, still built as stacked dwellings in brick with a flat roof, but now paired and served by a driveway, with the stairwell brought back inside and lit through glass block. The vocabulary is Art déco; the plan is a plex adjusting to the suburb.

What Évaluation du patrimoine urbain says about this type

Description (source)
  • 26.U.2 : « Les traits architecturaux de ces résidences d'inspiration Art déco sont : le parement de brique, le toit plat, l'entrée accentuée et protégée par un auvent de fer forgé suspendu à des chaînes, l'utilisation de blocs de verre pour éclairer la cage d'escalier intérieure, les motifs ornementaux en pierre artificielle (castor, écusson, etc.) et les balcons avec garde-corps en fer ornemental. »
  • 26.U.1 : « Ce petit secteur est composé de maisons jumelés de deux étages, en brique et à toit plat, et possédant un garage extérieur en fond de cour. »
Siting & landscape
  • Semi-detached rather than contiguous — paired buildings separated from the next pair by a driveway running to a garage at the back of the lot.
  • Set back slightly from the street, allowing a minimal arrangement of lawn and rows of shrubs.
  • In 26.U.1 the street layout is made of short segments that interrupt the regular grid of the avenues, and mature trees stand in the front yards.
  • The uniformity of the buildings in each sector led the cahier to infer a single promoter in both cases.
Massing
  • Two storeys, flat roof.
  • The garage is off the street entirely, at the far end of the lot, reached by the shared driveway; the front is left to the entrance.
Articulation
  • Entrance emphasised and sheltered by a wrought-iron marquise hung on chains.
  • Ornamental motifs in artificial stone — the cahier names a beaver and an escutcheon.
  • Balconies with ornamental iron railings.
Openings
  • Glass block used to light the interior stairwell — the clearest sign that the stair here is inside, as in the parc Campbell duplex, not climbing the front.
Materials
  • Brick facing, with artificial stone for the ornamental motifs; wrought and ornamental iron for the marquise and the railings; glass block.

Assembled from two sector fiches, both quoted in full in profile_fr; the richer Art déco vocabulary belongs to 26.U.2 and the plainer paired-house description to 26.U.1, and the variants field keeps them apart rather than merging them into an average. Dwelling counts are the cahier's own words — 26.U.2 says « duplex jumelés » and 26.U.1 says « maisons jumelés », so the type is recorded as a duplex on 26.U.2's authority and 26.U.1 is carried as the plainer variant. No dimensions are given in either fiche.

The exterior stair — why it is outside

Variants the source distinguishes
  • 26.U.1, north of Saint-Zotique between the 6e and 8e Avenue: semi-detached houses of two storeys in brick with a flat roof and a detached garage at the back of the yard; short street segments interrupting the grid of the avenues, mature trees in the front yards. A single promoter, in the 1950s.
  • 26.U.2, near the Centre civique: semi-detached duplex separated by driveways to garages at the back of the lot, with the fuller Art déco vocabulary set out below. A single promoter, 1940s–1950s.
