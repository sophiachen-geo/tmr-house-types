Two to three storeys under a mansard whose two tin finishes are specified separately — standing seam on the flat deck above, Canadian pan on the steep slope below. Four- and six-pane casements, gabled or round-headed dormers, a covered veranda and carpentered trim.

Where it comes from

At about 450 buildings, one of the four big currents in the catalogue, and the form of the Lévis of the railway decades after 1870. The old hôtel de ville, demolished in 1965 and shown in the City's walking-tour brochure on the site of the present parc Joseph-Godéric-Blanchet, was Second Empire.

What Ville de Lévis says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • The City counts about 450 buildings of this current across the merged territory of Lévis (fiche « Nombre à Lévis »).
Massing
  • Two to three storeys.
  • Rectangular plan.
  • Mansard roof of two or four slopes, standing-seam tin on the flat upper deck (terrasson) and Canadian-pan tin on the steep lower slope (brisis).
  • Chimney; covered veranda.
  • 2 à 3 étages
  • cheminée, galerie couverte
  • rectangulaire
  • mansardée, à 2 ou à 4 versants, tôle à baguettes sur le terrasson et tôle à la canadienne sur le brisis
Articulation
  • Window and door surrounds, corner boards, decorative woodwork and veranda trim.
  • chambranles, planches cornières, boiseries décoratives et garnitures de galerie
Openings
  • Casement windows of four and six panes and, more rarely, sash windows; gabled or round-headed dormers.
  • fenêtre à battants à 4 et 6 carreaux et, plus rarement, à guillotine, lucarnes à pignon ou ceintrées
Materials
  • Brick, wood clapboard or wood shingle.
  • brique, clin ou bardeau de bois
