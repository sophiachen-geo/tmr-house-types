The garden-city enclaves: brick semi-detached houses on curving streets, built in the mid-1920s at Douglas and Grenville and at Priests' Farm, which straddles the Montréal boundary.

Where it comes from

The Garden City idea reaching Westmount a decade after it built Mount Royal, and in the same Arts and Crafts vocabulary — which is why this record and Mount Royal's Garden City House share a canonical form.

What Ville de Montréal says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • Douglas and Grenville (23.E.4), curvilinear streets laid out by the architect W. S. Wright on garden-city principles, mid-1920s; Priests' Farm (23.E.11), built 1926–1930 by L. E. Schlemm to plans by Shorey and Ritchie, « l'un des premiers projets canadiens à adopter les rues sinueuses ».
Massing
  • Semi-detached houses; at Priests' Farm, flat roofs to the south and steep pitches to the north.
