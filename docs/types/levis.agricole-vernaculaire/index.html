The rural building of the merged territory: a long rectangle of vertical boarding or shingle under a straight two-slope roof, sometimes mansarded, with big hinged or track-hung doors, a roof vent, lean-to additions and a silo.

Where it comes from

Rural rather than urban, and included here because Lévis's 2002 merger of ten municipalities brought a large farming territory inside one city boundary — Saint-Étienne, Saint-Nicolas, Pintendre, Breakeyville and Vire-Crêpes all carry PIIA heritage sectors of this kind. The RPCQ's cited site at Saint-Nicolas is itself described as « un ensemble continu de propriétés agricoles ». About a hundred are counted.

Siting & landscape
  • The City counts about 100 buildings of this current across the merged territory of Lévis (fiche, « Nombre à Lévis »).
Massing
  • One and a half to two and a half storeys.
  • Rectangular plan.
  • Straight two-slope roof, sometimes mansarded, in wood shingle or barn tin.
  • Roof vent, lean-to volumes, silo.
  • 1 ½ à 2 ½ étages
  • évent, volumes en appentis, silo
  • rectangulaire
  • à 2 versants droits, parfois mansardé, en bardeau de bois ou en tôle de grange
Articulation
  • Sober; window and door surrounds; a weathervane.
  • sobre, chambranles, girouette
Openings
  • Large hinged or sliding (track-hung) doors; windows with large panes.
  • grandes portes à battants ou coulissantes (sur rail), fenêtres à grands carreaux
Materials
  • Vertical wood boarding or wood shingle.
  • planches de bois verticales ou bardeau de bois
