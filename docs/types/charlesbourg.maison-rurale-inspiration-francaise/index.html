Rural house of French inspiration La maison rurale d'inspiration française

Photograph pendingPlan de conservation du site patrimonial de Charlesbourg (2016), fig. 28 — the plan's own photograph of the maison Éphraïm-Bédard, the example it prints for this type. © Gouvernement du Québec, ministère de la Culture et des Communications. Not reproduced.

The oldest house in the site and the only one that ignores the road: a low, squat, one-and-a-half storey box of pièce-sur-pièce or whitewashed rendered stone, under two straight steep slopes of wood shingle, with one or two stacks and small-paned casements set wherever the inside wanted them. Its façade faces south for the sun, whatever direction the Trait-Carré happens to run.

Where it comes from

The house of the Régime français, built on the trapezoidal farm lots the Jesuits conceded from 1665 and, under the règle du tiers, always in the first third of the lot against the Trait-Carré. The plan de conservation warns that this type is easier to see than to date: several 18th-century houses were altered so heavily that their apparent age is nothing like their real one — 8180-8182 Le Trait-Carré Est was built about 1724 and given a mansard roof in 1931 — while the maison Éphraïm-Bédard looks like a French-inspired farmhouse and was built about a century later.

What MCC says about this type

Description (source)
  • La maison rurale d'inspiration française est le plus ancien type d'habitation que l'on trouve sur le territoire du site patrimonial. Ce type s'est adapté aux conditions locales particulières, telles que le climat, les savoir-faire et les matériaux disponibles.
Siting & landscape
  • Generally set without regard to the public road, its principal façade turned south for the most sunlight — the sharpest siting rule in this part, and the opposite of Île d'Orléans, where the chemin Royal governs.
  • The oldest dwelling type in the site. Survivors stand mainly on the ceinture, the old farm lots outside the Trait-Carré.
  • Ce type de bâtiment est généralement implanté sans égard à la voie publique et sa façade principale est orientée vers le sud pour bénéficier d'un maximum d'ensoleillement.
Massing
  • Almost square or rectangular plan, of modest dimensions, one and a half storeys.
  • Barely raised off the ground, with squat proportions.
  • Two straight steeply pitched slopes, covered in wood shingle.
  • un plan presque carré ou rectangulaire. Des dimensions modestes et un volume d'un étage et demi. La maison est peu exhaussée du sol et ses proportions sont trapues
  • un toit à deux versants droits à forte pente, couvert en bardeaux de bois
Articulation
  • The plan lists no ornament for this type at all — the whole description is plan, roof, walls, openings and chimneys.
Openings
  • Asymmetrically disposed; wood casement windows with small panes.
  • des ouvertures disposées de façon asymétrique. Ces fenêtres en bois sont à battants à petits carreaux
Materials
  • Walls of pièce-sur-pièce or of stone, the stone usually rendered and whitewashed.
  • One or two chimney stacks emerging from the roof.
  • des murs en pièce sur pièce ou en pierre (habituellement recouverte d'un crépi et chaulée)
  • une ou deux souches de cheminées émergent de sa toiture

Transcribed from the plan de conservation's printed p. 48. The French under each column is the plan's own wording, unedited: the five bullets of « Voici les principales caractéristiques de ce type » plus the two sentences of the introductory paragraph, which carry the siting rule and the type's position in the sequence. The plan supplies no ornament, no window proportion and no roof pitch in degrees for this type, so those cells stay empty rather than being filled by inference.
