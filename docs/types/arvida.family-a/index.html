Family A company houses (models A1–A8) Maisons de compagnie de la famille A (modèles A1 à A8)

Photograph pendingpending: the model A1 catalogue page is published by arvida.saguenay.ca, whose legal notice reserves all rights (see sources/arvida/MANIFEST.md); not reproduced here

The first-phase house: one of the standard models of the summer of 1926, re-drawn by Wake to fit French-Canadian family life (a big kitchen instead of parlour and dining room), varied from house to house by dormers, porches and roof details so that, in the City's phrase, "not a single house was built that resembles its neighbour".

Where it comes from

Brainerd's New York-inspired type plans were "rationalised" on site by the engineer Harold R. Wake, who adapted them to Québec's climate and household size; the CPCQ dossier speaks of "quatre modèles types" enabling the 270 houses of 1926 in 135 days, with variety obtained through dormers, balconies and roofs. The address list is consistent with families A–D being that first set: on the 1926 streets almost every entry is an A, B, C or D code.

What Ville de Saguenay says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • Set back on the curving tree-lined streets of the 1926 core around parc Oersted (rues Davy, Deville, Moritz, Hare, Wohler, Oersted, Moissan, Edison, Faraday, boulevard Mellon, rue Lavoisier).
  • Concrete foundations from the outset ("a symbol of permanence").
Massing
  • Model A1 (documented): a detached house whose roofline includes dormers; plan reorganised under Harold R. Wake so that a kitchen-family room replaces the separate dining and living rooms and adjoins two ground-floor bedrooms.
