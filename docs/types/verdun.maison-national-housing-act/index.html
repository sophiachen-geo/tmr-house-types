National Housing Act house — 1½ storeys, brick, to prize-winning plans L'habitation unifamiliale de masse « National Housing Act »

A line of 1½-storey brick houses on rue LeClair in Crawford Park, Verdun — uniform heights, uniform roof pitches, planted front yards. Photograph by Commons user A611662, CC BY-SA 4.0. Licence: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0. File: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Crawford_Park_Extension.jpg

A storey and a half, brick, detached on its own lot, one of several hundred built to the same handful of award-winning plans on streets that curve away from Verdun's grid. The 2005 evaluation ranks the result among the borough's seven sectors of exceptional value — for the ensemble, not for the house.

Where it comes from

Crawford Park is Verdun's one piece of planned suburb, and the cahier treats it as an event in the history of housing policy rather than as architecture: the federal National Housing Act « quelques années avant la Seconde Guerre mondiale » produced « de nouvelles typologies d'habitation … et plus particulièrement l'habitation unifamiliale de masse », and Crawford is « un exemple exceptionnel de ce nouveau type d'intervention urbaine ». What is exceptional, in the document's own reasoning, is uniformity plus the abandoned grid — heights, materials, roof pitches, siting and landscaping all held constant on curving streets, in a borough otherwise laid out in blocks. The same house recurs twice more in Verdun and the cahier separates the three occurrences by street plan alone: 21.I.9 puts it back on the orthogonal grid around a park, and 21.I.10 finds it dropped onto conventionally subdivided lots in the south. This is the same form as the Lévis wartime housing and the Sud-Ouest's maison de vétérans, and it is also the only house type anywhere on this site that a municipal fee schedule names: Verdun's 2020 tariff by-law sets a PIIA study fee reserved for enlarging the roof volume of a "wartime"-type house to add habitable space.

What Ville de Montréal says about this type

Description (source)
  • « Quelques années avant la Seconde Guerre mondiale, le gouvernement promulgue le « National Housing Act ». De nouvelles typologies d'habitation prendront forme dans ce cadre, et plus particulièrement l'habitation unifamiliale de masse. Le voisinage Crawford offre un exemple exceptionnel de ce nouveau type d'intervention urbaine, constitué d'immeubles d'un étage et demi recouverts d'un parement de brique. Ces immeubles ont été construits sur la base de plans d'architecture primés. » (21.E.5, le voisinage Crawford)
Historical context (fiche)
  • « Suivant les mêmes typologies que dans le secteur Crawford, ce projet urbain est aménagé autour d'un parc, mais cette fois, inscrit dans la trame de rues orthogonale de la ville. » (21.I.9, un projet « National Housing Act »)
  • « La typologie « War Time » dans un projet intégré décrite précédemment s'est aussi implantée dans ce secteur, sur des terrains lotis préalablement selon une trame conventionnelle. Les immeubles sont généralement de bonne facture, avec parement de brique et(ou) de pierre et toiture plate avec mansardes ou toit à pignon. » (21.I.10, le voisinage Sud)
Siting & landscape
  • Detached houses on individual lots, set back behind a planted front yard, on a street layout that abandons the city's orthogonal grid — the cahier's last sentence for the sector is « On abandonne ici la trame de rues orthogonale. »
  • The homogeneity of the siting mode and of the landscaping is named, alongside the buildings, as a reason the ensemble is of exceptional value.
Massing
  • One and a half storeys throughout. Uniform heights and uniform roof pitches across the whole development.
Articulation
  • Not described. The cahier's interest is in the ensemble, not the elevation, and it names no ornament, no porch and no door treatment.
Openings
  • Not described.
Materials
  • Brick cladding, uniform across the development. 21.I.10 records the same "War Time" typology elsewhere in the borough with brick and/or stone.
Éléments caractéristiques (source)
  • « L'homogénéité des hauteurs, des matériaux, des pentes de toit, du mode d'implantation et du paysagement contribue à la qualité de l'ensemble. On abandonne ici la trame de rues orthogonale. » (21.E.5)

Assembled from sector paragraphs; the Évaluation publishes no type table. 21.E.5 gives storeys, cladding, roof-pitch uniformity, siting and street layout, and is the only place the cahier states the National Housing Act connection. 21.I.9 and 21.I.10 are quoted because they record the same house twice more in the borough and are what allow the roof form to be entered at all: 21.E.5 mentions « pentes de toit » without naming a form, and 21.I.10 says « toiture plate avec mansardes ou toit à pignon » of a mixed fabric that includes the War Time houses among others. `roof.form` is therefore entered as gabled with a null pitch, on 21.E.5's roof-pitch uniformity plus 21.I.10's « toit à pignon », and should be treated as the weakest field in this record. Articulation and openings are left empty rather than inferred from Wartime Housing Limited's standard plans, which the cahier does not cite. The « plans d'architecture primés » are not identified in the document and no architect is named; the Commons uploader of the photograph below attributes the Crawford Park layout to Samuel Gitterman at the NHA, which is not in any municipal source consulted here and is not asserted.
