The lower town's default building: a narrow greystone unit repeated up to eight times in a run, flat-roofed or crowned with a false mansard, its individuality carried entirely by the cornice and the painted woodwork of the entrance.

Where it comes from

Built 1870–1905 as Westmount urbanised the flat land below the escarpment; the same Montréal greystone that faces the Plateau's maisons urbaines, quarried from the cliff itself. Parks Canada's statement lists row houses among the components whose "diversity and integrity" make the district an exemplary Victorian and post-Victorian suburb.

What Évaluation du patrimoine urbain — Westmount (2005) says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • The flat lower town; buildings aligned to the street with shallow or no front garden; runs of up to eight units (sector 23.E.24, 1885–1905).
  • Hallowell-Weredale (23.E.33): about 240 buildings, mostly attached town houses of 1870–1914, the first Westmount project with paved rear lanes.
Massing
  • Two to three storeys, narrow party-wall units; flat roof or false-mansard crown.
Articulation
  • Repetitive bays; cornices; decorative wood joinery ("tarabiscotage"), polychrome and nearly intact at Place de Blenheim (23.E.31, sixteen identical façades).

  • Le calcaire local — 'la pierre grise de Montréal' — préféré à la brique dans ce secteur (23.E.24).
