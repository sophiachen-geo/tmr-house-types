Cubic house (Four Square) La maison cubique (ou « Four Square »)

Photograph pendingPlan de conservation du site patrimonial de Charlesbourg (2016), fig. 31 (first of three) — 723-725-727 boulevard Louis-XIV. © Gouvernement du Québec, ministère de la Culture et des Communications. Not reproduced.

A square plan two storeys high under a shallow pavilion roof, sometimes with a single dormer on the front slope, clad in horizontal boards or brick. The plan gives it three lines and no more — which is itself the point: the whole appeal of the industrial vernacular was that it needed no more than three lines to describe.

Where it comes from

The same American Foursquare that Saint-Lambert, Gatineau, Québec City, Trois-Rivières, Lévis and Île d'Orléans all catalogue. What differs at Charlesbourg is its rank: here it is a sub-variant inside the maison vernaculaire industrielle rather than a courant of its own, and the plan's built-fabric survey confirms it arrived with the century, putting most of the site's pavilion roofs on two-storey cubic houses of the first decades of the 20th century.

What MCC says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • The plan notes that most of the site's pavilion roofs sit on two-storey cubic houses built in the first decades of the 20th century.
Massing
  • Distinguished by a square plan of two storeys.
  • Low-pitched pavilion roof, sometimes with a dormer on the front slope.
  • un volume simplifié. Une charpente claire, aussi appelée « Balloon Frame »
Articulation
  • Inherited from the parent type — standardised architectural elements, doors and windows.
Openings
  • Rectangular openings of vertical proportion; wood windows, casement or sash.
  • des ouvertures de forme rectangulaire aux proportions verticales. Ces fenêtres en bois sont à battants ou à guillotine
Materials
  • Cladding often of horizontal boards or of brick.
  • des matériaux usinés, tels que les poutres et les planches. Des nouveaux matériaux faisant leur apparition au début du XXe siècle, comme la tuile d'amiante-ciment. Des éléments architecturaux standardisés, tels que les portes et les fenêtres
Sous-variantes
  • la maison cubique se distingue par un plan carré à deux étages. Sur son toit en pavillon à faible pente, on trouve parfois une lucarne sur le versant avant. Son parement est souvent en planches horizontales ou en brique

The sub-variant bullet is transcribed verbatim from printed p. 51; the volumetrie, ouvertures and materiaux entries are the parent type's shared bullets, repeated here so the child record stands on its own. The plan gives no roofing material and no pitch for the cubique.
