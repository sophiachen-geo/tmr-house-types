Village Boomtown house, two storeys, flat or low-pitch roof behind a crown L'architecture Boomtown

Avenue Cartier, Pointe-Claire — two-storey clapboard houses close on the street with two-level galleries on turned posts and sawn brackets, regular vertical openings and low or flat roofs. Photograph by Denis Tremblay for « Parcours riverain - Ville de Montréal », 18 October 2012, via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Avenue_Cartier_(Pointe-Claire)_(8738646295).jpg

Photograph pendingVille de Pointe-Claire, Code villageois PC-2880, chapitre 03, the figures captioned « Boomtown » and « Boomtown avec ornementations victoriennes ». The by-law's own illustrations of the type. © Ville de Pointe-Claire. Not reproduced — permission required.

Two storeys in a cubic volume under a flat or barely sloping roof, regular vertical openings that are usually sashes, a front kept free of projections except for the gallery under its awning, and all the ornament gathered at the top in a moulded cornice, a parapet or a pattern laid in the brick. It is the style the Code villageois names for seven of its ten landscape units, which makes it the ordinary building of Pointe-Claire's village.

Where it comes from

The Code ties it to a rebuilding. The traditional Québec architecture of the oldest sectors, it says, recalls the village as it was up to the fire; after the fire, two styles produce a certain homogeneity, and the Boomtown, "apparue au début du 20e siècle", is the first of them. That is the whole of the origin story the source gives, and it is worth noticing what kind of document gives it: not an inventory, not a conservation plan, but a zoning by-law written on form-based principles, which had to describe the village's architecture in order to prescribe more of it. The same form under the same name is carded on this site at Charlesbourg, where a ministerial plan de conservation files it as one of three sub-variants of the maison vernaculaire industrielle — and the Code villageois lists « vernaculaire industriel » as a separate style of units UP5 and UP8, alongside the Boomtown rather than above it.

What Ville de Pointe-Claire says about this type

Description (source)
  • L'architecture Boomtown, apparue au début du 20e siècle, est caractérisée par son volume cubique, ses deux étages et son toit plat ou à faible pente. Les ouvertures verticales sont distribuées de manière régulière (généralement des fenêtres à guillotine). On note une façade avec peu de saillies, à l'exception de la galerie couverte d'un auvent. L'ornementation se limite au couronnement par une corniche moulurée, un parapet ou un jeu de brique.
Historical context (fiche)
  • Après l'incendie de 1900, deux styles principaux créent une certaine homogénéité.
Siting & landscape
  • The dominant style of the village core and of both village entrances: the Code names it as the architectural style of units UP1, UP2, UP4, UP5, UP6, UP8 and UP10 — seven of the ten.
  • In UP1 and UP2 the setbacks are nil to narrow, front and side, and the coverage is about 50 %; in UP5 the front margin is « très faible » and the coverage about 40 %; in UP8 the front margin is deep and the coverage about 30 %. The same style, sited three different ways.
Massing
  • Cubic volume, two storeys, flat roof or one of low pitch.
  • The Code's general gabarit rule for the two-storey case: a carré of two storeys, with a ground floor and a first floor that occupy the whole footprint, under a flat roof or one of at least two slopes whose pitch is under 9/12.
  • les bâtiments dont le carré fait deux étages de hauteur, comprenant un rez-de-chaussée et un premier étage qui occupent la totalité du périmètre d'implantation, avec un toit plat ou un toit à au moins deux versants dont la pente est inférieure à 9/12
Articulation
  • A façade with few projections, apart from the gallery under its awning.
  • The ornament is confined to the crown: a moulded cornice, a parapet, or a pattern in the brick.
  • The Code adds that a bandeau or a crown is present on every heritage building in the village, whatever the style, and that this is what unifies the frontages.
  • Architectural details are generally sober; the principal ones are railings of simple vertical members and shutters matching the style of the building.
  • Un bandeau ou un couronnement est présent sur l'ensemble des bâtiments patrimoniaux. Malgré les styles différents, ce détail participe à unifier les façades et à préserver le cachet villageois.
  • La corniche moulurée, le parapet, la fausse mansarde ou le jeu de brique est présent sur les bâtiments à toit plat ou à faible pente.
  • Les détails architecturaux sont généralement sobres. Les principaux sont les garde-corps composés d'éléments verticaux simples et les volets de fenêtre s'apparentant au style du bâtiment.
Openings
  • Vertical openings, regularly distributed — generally sash windows.
  • Les ouvertures sont dans l'ensemble des styles existants distribuées de manière régulière et à la verticale.
Materials
  • The Code's rule for the whole village: brick and darker colours are typical of the chemin du Bord-du-Lac, while the other sectors often have clapboard or roughcast in light colours.
  • Unit by unit: brick, wood clapboard and roughcast in earthy and dark tones in UP1 and UP2; roughcast, wood clapboard and marine tones in UP5; light colours and earthy tones in UP6; brick and wood clapboard in varied colours in UP10.
  • Les matériaux et coloris varient quelque peu. La brique et des couleurs plus foncées sont typiques du chemin Bord-du-Lac alors que les autres secteurs ont souvent des déclins ou du crépi de couleurs claires.

Everything in the French column is verbatim from by-law PC-2880: the style paragraph of chapter 3, the two-storey clause of the same chapter's gabarit rule, and the numbered notes 1, 5, 6 and 7 on the facing page, which the Code states for all village styles rather than for this one. The Code gives no roof pitch in degrees — the 9/12 threshold in the gabarit rule is a maximum for the two-storey case, not a measured pitch — no lot width, no setback in metres, no roofing material and no rule about garages, so those cells are empty. Two cautions. First, this is a form-based code: its statements are prescriptions for what should be built in the village as much as descriptions of what stands there, and the per-unit rows in the siting and materials columns are drawn from the unités paysagères tables, which are explicitly prescriptive. Second, the Code dates the style to "le début du 20e siècle" and ties its arrival to the village fire, whose year it gives as 1900 twice and 1901 once; the phase is p3 on the 1900 reading.

Variants the source distinguishes
  • Boomtown avec ornementations victoriennes : The Code illustrates a fourth figure beside the three styles and captions it « Boomtown avec ornementations victoriennes ». It gives no separate description, so nothing is transcribed for it beyond the name; it is recorded because the Code prints it as a distinct thing.
