The picturesque villas and country cottages of the slope, standing in their own grounds before the street grid reached them: Metcalfe Terrace (1840), Braemar and Riverview (1847).

Where it comes from

The first houses built for pleasure rather than farming on the Côte-Saint-Antoine lands, whose rural antecedent — the Maison Hurtubise of 1739, classified in 2004 — still stands on the founding road. Braemar (1847, 3219 The Boulevard) was recognised in 1984.

What Ville de Montréal says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • Free-standing houses in their own grounds along the chemin de la Côte-Saint-Antoine (23.E.14) and at Rosemount (23.E.8), the 'berceau de Westmount' of about 1874, at low density and with deep setbacks.
