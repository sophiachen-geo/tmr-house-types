Company workers' house of the Hudon mill, rue Saint-Germain La maison ouvrière de la filature Hudon, rue Saint-Germain

Photograph pendingpending — no verified free image of the rue Saint-Germain row was found on Wikimedia Commons

The corporate counterpart to Maisonneuve, built two years before Maisonneuve existed. Brick-faced wooden cubes running unbroken along the sidewalk on rue Saint-Germain, two dwellings each with the stair inside, put up in 1881 by the V. Hudon cotton mill for its own workers — and, according to the Ville's MEM, the only surviving nineteenth-century company-built housing in Montréal.

Where it comes from

Hochelaga industrialised faster than it could house anyone. Four large works arrived between 1871 and 1881 — the W.C. McDonald tobacco factory, the Abattoirs de l'Est, the Compagnie des moulins à coton V. Hudon and the Filature Sainte-Anne, the last two merging in 1905 into Dominion Textile, which then held half of the Canadian textile industry — and, in the Évaluation's words, « les terrains sont lotis sans réglementation particulière ». The Hudon mill announced its own workers' housing in early December 1881 and built this row; it was renovated in 1981, exactly a century later. Read this record against the plex on the next page and against Arvida and Témiscaming: an employer housing its workforce directly is what a company town does, and it is precisely what Maisonneuve two years later did not do. Maisonneuve's promoters sold lots and built civic monuments instead, and left the housing to the ordinary speculative builder.

What MEM says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • Built at the sidewalk edge with no setback, and set in an unbroken run — « disposées en enfilade et en bordure du trottoir ».
  • Rue Saint-Germain between rues Rouville and Adam, civic numbers 1451 to 1544; one of the three streets sector 27.E.1 names as the first ground of Hochelaga's workers' housing.
Massing
  • Square-ish two-storey volumes — « cubes de bois revêtus de briques ».
  • Two dwellings, one on the ground floor and one above, reached by a stair inside the building. No exterior stair; this is 1881, nearly twenty years before the outside stair becomes standard in Montréal.

Siting, massing and materials are quoted from the MEM (Mémoires des Montréalais / Centre des mémoires montréalaises, Ville de Montréal) article of 8 November 2017 by the Atelier d'histoire Mercier-Hochelaga-Maisonneuve, which is the only account of these houses found in an institutional source; the Évaluation names rue Saint-Germain as a first street of workers' housing but gives no fiche and does not list these buildings individually. Articulation and openings are left empty rather than inferred — no photograph of the row could be verified on Commons, and the houses were renovated in 1981, so present appearance is not evidence of 1881 detailing. The interior stair is the article's own statement, « L'escalier est à l'intérieur », and it is the reason this record attaches to duplex-interior-stair rather than to the exterior-stair members of the plex family.
