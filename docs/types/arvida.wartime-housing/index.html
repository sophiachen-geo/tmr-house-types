The federal Crown corporation's prefab, dropped by the hundred around the smelter in the war years and then bought, jacked up and given proper foundations by the company in 1949 so they would stay.

Where it comes from

Wartime Housing Limited (1941–47) built temporary workers' housing across Canada; in Arvida the aluminium company, "interested in conserving, or even developing a dense image of the city of aluminum", acquired most of them and made them permanent.

What Ville de Saguenay says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • More than 500 built in Arvida during the war; most acquired by the company; in 1949 lifted and set on concrete foundations.
Massing
  • Single-family, minimalist; frame, roof and walls pre-cut and prefabricated in panels so the house could be dismantled and re-erected elsewhere.
  • Originally on cedar posts (pillars), later on concrete foundations.
