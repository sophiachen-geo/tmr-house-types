One and a half storeys sitting almost on the ground under a steep two-slope or hipped roof with barely any eaves overhang. Walls are massive stone, sometimes rendered or boarded over. Windows are few, small-paned casements, set without symmetry; a large stone chimney is the only projection.

Where it comes from

The oldest of the twenty currents in the City's catalogue, covering the French colonial period on the seigneurie de Lauzon, granted to Jean de Lauson in 1636 and settled at Pointe-Lévy from 1647. The City counts about fifteen buildings of this kind across the whole merged territory — the smallest surviving group in the catalogue, and the reason so little of Lévis reads as pre-Conquest today.

What Ville de Lévis says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • The City counts about 15 buildings of this current across the merged territory of Lévis (fiche « Nombre à Lévis »).
Massing
  • One and a half storeys, sitting very low to the ground.
  • Rectangular plan.
  • Two-slope or hipped roof of steep pitch, with a barely projecting eaves overhang, covered in wood shingle.
  • One or more large stone chimneys.
  • 1 ½ étage très peu dégagé du sol
  • large(s) cheminée(s) en pierre
  • rectangulaire
  • à 2 versants ou à croupe, à pente forte, avant-toit peu débordant, revêtement de bardeau de bois
Articulation
  • Plain window and door surrounds; stripped-back decoration.
  • chambranles simples, décor dépouillé
Openings
  • Casement windows with small panes, few in number and asymmetrically placed; small dormers added in the 19th century.
  • fenêtres à battants à petits carreaux, rares et asymétriques, petites lucarnes ajoutées au 19e siècle
Materials
  • Massive stone walls, sometimes rendered in stucco or clad in wood clapboard or vertical boarding.
  • murs massifs en pierre, parfois revêtus de crépi, de clin de bois ou de planches verticales
