Row duplex of the 1950s Le duplex en rangée d'après 1950

No freely licensed photograph of the voisinage Ouest row duplexes was identified on Commons.

Two flats stacked and built in a row, on the strip between the aqueduct canal and rue Bannantyne that was the last ground left. Post-1950, from a handful of repeated designs, and valued by the evaluation for the quality of what it was originally made of.

Where it comes from

Verdun ran out of land in the late 1950s — the cahier says the development « se poursuivra de façon constante pour atteindre les limites du territoire à la fin des années 1950 », after which « l'expansion se poursuivra alors sur le seul territoire encore vierge, l'île des Sœurs ». The voisinage Ouest is the end of that sequence on the mainland, and it shows the plex having quietly changed shape: no longer three storeys with a stair on the front, but two stacked dwellings repeated in a row from a short catalogue of models. Nothing about the transition is explained by the document, which is the honest state of the record; what it does say is that the value lies in the materials and in the harmony, and that where the original subdivision survived it produced public green space. Read alongside the exterior-stair plex of the 1900–1920 wave and the balcony duplex of the south, it completes the borough's three-stage plex sequence within one small territory.

What Ville de Montréal says about this type

Description (source)
  • « Ce secteur situé entre le canal de l'aqueduc et la rue Bannantyne s'est construit principalement après 1950. Ce sont majoritairement des duplex en rangée reprenant quelques modèles originaux. L'intérêt de ce secteur découle de la qualité des matériaux d'origine et de l'harmonie générale. Certains îlots bénéficient aussi de la morphologie des lotissements d'origine, qui ont entraîné la planification d'espaces verts publics. » (21.I.8, le voisinage Ouest)
Historical context (fiche)
  • « De l'Hôpital Douglas à la limite de la municipalité, on retrouve un cadre bâti assez hétérogène de basse densité. … Le secteur est essentiellement résidentiel (unifamilial ou bifamilial), avec des immeubles majoritairement détachés sur des terrains individuels. … Ce sont des constructions un peu plus récentes que celles du reste de l'arrondissement. » (21.I.10, le voisinage Sud)
Siting & landscape
  • In rows, on the last buildable ground in the borough — between the aqueduct canal and rue Bannantyne — filled in mainly after 1950.
  • Some blocks keep the shape of the original subdivision, and the cahier credits that shape with having forced public green space into the plan.
Massing
  • Two dwellings stacked, built in a row rather than paired or free-standing. The cahier says the row works « quelques modèles originaux » — a small number of repeated designs.

The most sparsely evidenced record on this page, and included because it closes Verdun's plex sequence rather than because the source says much. Everything comes from one four-sentence fiche, 21.I.8, which is a secteur de valeur intéressante — hence `sectors: null`, since the ten 21.I codes are not encoded in this part. That fiche gives the date, the row form, the dwelling count implied by "duplex", and a judgement about materials; it gives no storey count, no roof form, no cladding, no opening description and no ornament. `storeys: 2`, `roof.form: flat` and `principal_cladding` are entered from the word "duplex" and from the borough's uniform practice as the cahier describes it elsewhere, not from this fiche, and are the fields to distrust. Articulation and openings are left empty. The canonical link is to the plex family as a whole rather than to one of its seven children, because nothing in the source says where the stair to the upper dwelling is.
