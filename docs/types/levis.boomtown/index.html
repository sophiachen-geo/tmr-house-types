One to two storeys, square or rectangular, with the roof either flat or falling so gently to the rear that the street sees a straight line. A parapet or cornice finishes the front, a covered veranda fronts it, and corner boards and surrounds do the rest.

Where it comes from

The same form Saint-Lambert, Gatineau, Québec City and Trois-Rivières each describe in their own words — six independent municipal descriptions of one building. Lévis's fiche is the tersest of them and the only one that pins the shallow pitch to the rear slope explicitly. About a hundred are counted.

What Ville de Lévis says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • The City counts about 100 buildings of this current across the merged territory of Lévis (fiche « Nombre à Lévis »).
Massing
  • One to two storeys.
  • Rectangular or square plan.
  • Flat, or of very shallow pitch falling to the rear.
  • Parapet or cornice; covered veranda.
  • 1 à 2 étages
  • parapet ou corniche, galerie couverte
  • rectangulaire ou carré
  • plate ou à très faible pente vers l'arrière
Articulation
  • Corner boards and window surrounds; cornice elements.
  • planches cornières et chambranles, éléments de corniche
Openings
  • Multi-paned sash windows, symmetrically arranged.
  • fenêtres à guillotine à carreaux, disposées symétriquement
Materials
  • Wood clapboard or wood shingle; diamond-pattern asbestos-cement tiles.
  • clin ou bardeau de bois, tuiles d'amiante-ciment en losange
