Detached urban house La maison urbaine isolée

Photograph pendingpending

The bourgeois villa of the Plateau's first decades: a free-standing stone house set back from the street, individually designed, now rare and often converted.

Where it comes from

Built for the well-to-do west of Saint-Laurent and south of avenue des Pins, then along Sherbrooke, Square Saint-Louis and Saint-Hubert; the family also includes a two-unit superposed variant that differs from the duplex by storeys per dwelling, uniform stone facing and richer ornament.

What PIIA 2004-22 Annexe D says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • Front setback that may exceed 5 metres; small side setbacks of 1 to 2 metres.
  • Oldest examples (1850–1880) on rue Sherbrooke; from the 1900s, rarely, on rue Saint-Hubert, rue Rachel and avenue du Parc-La Fontaine. Many converted to offices or flats.
  • Marge de recul avant pouvant atteindre plus de 5 mètres, marges de recul latérales faibles de 1 à 2 mètres.
Massing
  • Centred door; a projecting element may form an oriel or forebody. No sub-variant.

Materials
  • Uniform stone cladding and a higher degree of ornamentation than the duplex (family description).
