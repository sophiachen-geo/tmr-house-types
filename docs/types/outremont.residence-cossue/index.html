The bourgeois house on the mountain flank, in whichever past the owner preferred.

Where it comes from

Édouard Gohier's Northmount Land Company began subdividing the flank in 1906, and the 1920s and 1930s filled it. The 2005 evaluation makes no attempt to reduce the result to a type: it records a sampling of revival styles on lots whose shape the slope dictated. 728 avenue Pratt, néoclassique, 1937, is one documented example.

What Ville de Montréal says about this type

Siting & landscape
  • The mountain flank, the steepest ground in the borough; lots vary in size because the streets are winding, sometimes switchbacking to avoid the steepest slopes.
  • The houses along chemin de la Côte-Sainte-Catherine are perched on the upper part of their lots and reached by long staircases.
  • Streets lined with numerous trees, which the evaluation reads as the source of the sector's picturesque character.
  • The part south of boulevard du Mont-Royal falls inside the Mount Royal heritage and natural district.
Massing
  • Detached, on large irregular lots; the topography « a favorisé les prouesses architecturales et paysagères ».
Articulation
  • A sampling of styles evocative of the past, alongside the new twentieth-century ones.
Openings
  • –
Materials
  • Noble materials — stone and brick.
