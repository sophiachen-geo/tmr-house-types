The one archival photograph of Rosemont duplexes with a verifiable licence — Conrad Poirier's of 28 July 1948 — was examined and then assigned to the SEMI-DETACHED Art déco duplex record instead, because what it actually shows is paired houses with all their doors at ground level under marquises, which is the 26.U.2 form and not this one. Publishing it here would have illustrated an exterior-stair type with a photograph containing no exterior stair.

Two flats stacked behind a brick front with an outside stair to the upper door — the smaller and commoner member of the same family as the triplex, and the form that fills the streets between the sectors the cahier singles out.

Where it comes from

The same subdivision and the same decades as the triplex: La Petite-Patrie from 1885 with the tramway, Rosemont from 1904 with the Angus Shops, and the building boom of the 1920s that made Rosemont one of the most active quarters in Montréal for house construction. Where the shoebox represents what a labourer could build for himself on a promise of sale, the duplex represents what a builder could put up for rent on the same street in the same years.

What Évaluation du patrimoine urbain says about this type

Description (source)
  • 26.I.2 : « hauteur variant de un à trois étages, mais majoritairement des duplex et triplex; parement extérieur en brique, toits plats, escaliers extérieurs et balcons, couronnements décoratifs et insertions de pierre artificielle pour enjoliver les façades sur rue. »
  • 26.E.5 : « le bon état des résidences (en majorité des duplex ou triplex contigus) qui bordent le parc »
Siting & landscape
  • Contiguous, at or close to the property line, in rows; enfilades of duplex and triplex are what the cahier says predominates in the older sectors.
  • Rear lanes to the back of the lots in the Rosemont Land grid.
Massing
  • Two storeys, two stacked dwellings, flat roof.
  • Exterior stair to the upper door, with balconies.
Articulation
  • Decorative couronnements and cornices; artificial-stone inserts on the street façade.
  • Serially produced decorative elements repeated along the row.
Openings
  • Regular arrangement of openings; tall vertical windows.
Materials
  • Brick as the exterior facing, with stone used more sparingly.

Same caveat as the triplex record: the cahier describes this type only inside sector fiches. The duplex and the triplex are almost always named together in those fiches — « des duplex ou triplex contigus » — so the two records share their descriptive material, and the difference between them here is dwelling count and storeys, which is exactly how the plex family is defined. Nothing has been imported from another borough's fiches to fill the gaps; lot width, setback and dwelling dimensions are left null.
