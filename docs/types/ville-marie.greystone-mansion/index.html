Ravenscrag, 835-1025 avenue des Pins Ouest — the Allan house, freestanding on its wooded ground below the mountain, with the belvedere tower of its Italianate composition. Photograph by Thomas1313, 7 August 2013, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Maison_Hugh_Allan,_dite_Ravenscrag_16.jpg — licence, author and file URL read through the Commons API before download; the photograph was opened and checked against its description.

The richest domestic architecture on the island of Montréal, and the least protected. A freestanding house of two and a half or three storeys standing in its own garden with its own outbuildings, on the slope between Sherbrooke and the mountain, built between about 1860 and 1935 in whichever revival was current — Italianate, Second Empire, Romanesque, Queen Anne, Beaux-Arts. The Ville grades the two sectors that hold them exceptionnelle. Neither carries an ensemble designation, and the columns below are empty because no document describes these houses as a type.

Where it comes from

The cahier's historical chapter puts it plainly: the north-west of the quartier Saint-Antoine, part of it running up the flank of the mountain, went on attracting the great Montréal bourgeoisie, which in the wake of the McTavishes and the McGills had sumptuous residences built by well-known architects — « On y compte les Allan, les Van Horne, les Forget » — and the sector would later be called the Square Mile. Héritage Montréal dates the golden age 1840–1930 and the buildings principally 1850–1930, and describes a commercial aristocracy that controlled two-thirds of the country's wealth. After the 1929 crash the families moved west to Westmount and the Town of Mount Royal, apartment and office buildings were put up in their gardens, and the quarter went, in Héritage Montréal's phrase, from golden age to urban decay in fifty years. Attrition, as English Wikipedia states it citing Rémillard and Merrett's Mansions of the Golden Square Mile (1987): "By 1983, only 30% of the mansions in the northern half of the Square Mile had survived demolition; and only 5% survived south of Sherbrooke Street." Héritage Montréal's own page carries a more cautious version — "Only about 30% of the bourgeois homes survived, north of Sherbrooke Street" — with neither the year nor the 5%. Whichever figure is right, the surviving stock is a remnant, and Héritage Montréal states that the ensemble "does not have a designation or specific strategy to protect and promote its heritage over current urban development considerations".

What RPCQ id 93528 (MCC); Ville de Montréal says about this type

Description (source)
  • « Compris dans l’arrondissement historique et naturel du mont Royal, ce secteur résidentiel est urbanisé entre 1860 et 1935. C’est un quartier de résidences bourgeoises souvent entourées de jardins et de dépendances. »
  • « On y retrouve un grand nombre de demeures bourgeoises exceptionnelles, dont certaines sont classées monuments historiques. On y voit notamment des résidences unifamiliales isolées, de luxueuses maisons en rangée et de riches immeubles de logements. »
  • « Certaines des villas sont aujourd’hui occupées par des bureaux, ambassades, consulats et délégations commerciales. Cette zone est caractérisée par la présence de bâtiments d’influence française et anglaise. »
Siting & landscape
  • Freestanding, often surrounded by gardens and outbuildings, on the south flank of the mountain.
  • The cahier values the sector as much for what the houses leave open as for the houses — « le dégagement qu'elle assure à la montagne ».
  • Only the part north of avenue du Docteur-Penfield falls inside the site patrimonial du Mont-Royal; sector 24.E.30, south of Sherbrooke, is explicitly outside it.
Massing
  • Not stated for this sector. The neighbouring croissant Redpath, 24.E.28, is described as mostly three storeys under a two-slope roof — that is the cahier's figure for that sector, not for this one.
Articulation
  • Not stated. The cahier calls the sector remarkable for « la qualité architecturale des bâtiments qui le composent » without describing that architecture.
Openings
  • Not stated.
Materials
  • Not stated for the sector. The cahier's neighbouring fiche 24.E.28 gives clay brick or stone for the Redpath crescent, and the site as a whole is characterised by grey limestone to 1850 and a wide variety of stone types after it.

Storeys, roof, openings, articulation, materials, lot width, setbacks and green front yard are all null, and the reason is the point of this record. Ville-Marie's own heritage document grades the Mille carré doré exceptionnelle and then describes it in six sentences, none of which describes a building: it gives the dates, the siting in gardens, the present institutional uses and the view of the mountain. There is no per-type table, no inventory fiche and no plan de conservation for this quarter, because it has no ensemble protection to generate one. The richest domestic architecture on the island is also the worst documented at type level of anything on this site, and the empty cells below are that fact rendered as data. Style list is the range of currents the named examples carry, attributed building by building in the list of buildings, not a characterisation either source makes of the sector.
